## Claim

Your board pack has a full treatise on how an OKR should be defined, but nowhere states an actual key result or who owns it. If an experienced board member can't find the target and the owner, the rest of the organization won't either — complexity is substituting for clarity, not demonstrating rigor.

## Source quote or paraphrase

Partner describes a real board pack: extensive explanation of what an objective/key-result *should* look like, but no actual key result stated, and the owner buried somewhere in the document. Reaction, paraphrased: if I can't find it as a fairly sharp outside board member, the rest of the organization definitely won't either.

## Why (inferred from source if not explicit)

Directly parallel to the existing "80-100 slide board deck" warning sign in this same research thread (atom-659/c-015 in the shareholder-reporting entry) — volume and procedural elaboration are a common founder substitute for the harder work of actually deciding and stating the target and the owner. The partner's specific test — "if I, a reasonably smart outsider, can't extract it, neither can the team" — is a usable litmus test in its own right, distinct from deck length.
