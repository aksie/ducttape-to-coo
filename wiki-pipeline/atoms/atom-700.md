## Claim

When board composition allows it, expect your investor to hand their own board seat to a trusted, neutral industry expert after a year or two rather than sitting on it indefinitely themselves — and treat that person, not the investor, as your natural first call.

## Source quote or paraphrase

Fund's stated board philosophy: keep the board small (ideally three, at most one shareholder seat), prefer an external chair, and increasingly aim to serve only 1-2 years before installing an industry expert who reports back to the fund. Explicitly: founders go to that person faster than to the investor, partly for their operating experience, but mainly because the investor's economic stake creates a structural filter founders know is there.

## Why (inferred from source if not explicit)

Two distinct mechanisms given: (1) leverage — a small number of partners can cover a large portfolio (this fund: 7 people, 45 companies) only if some board seats are delegated to trusted outsiders; (2) trust — an investor sitting on a board is legally obligated to act in the company's interest, but founders still perceive (correctly) an economic conflict that a neutral third party doesn't carry, so bad news and half-formed problems surface to the neutral party first.
