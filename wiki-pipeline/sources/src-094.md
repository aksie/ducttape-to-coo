## Summary

TechCrunch guest post by a practitioner who has sat on both sides of the table (as CEO reporting to a board, and as an independent director sitting on others), who says they resigned from two board seats specifically because they could not sit through another dysfunctional board meeting. Argues the deck itself is the problem: replacing an 80-slide deck with a short written memo (illustrated via a real example from a company called TigerEye, written after their Series A) produces shorter, higher-value 60-minute meetings instead of unfocused three-hour ones. The tone is explicitly critical of standard board-meeting practice, giving it adversarial/counter-narrative value in this corpus.

## Notable claims

- Replacing a large slide deck with a concise (roughly 3-page) written memo, read in advance, is what actually gets a board meeting down to 60 focused minutes instead of three unstructured hours
- The author resigned from board seats specifically because sitting through repeated low-value board meetings was not worth it — a direct signal of what board dysfunction costs even the people running the meeting, not just the founder
- A well-run early-stage board meeting should be mostly conversation and decision-making, not a read-through of the numbers the board could have absorbed from a pre-read
- Founders can set the format of their own board meetings — the deck-heavy, presentation-style norm is a default, not a requirement
