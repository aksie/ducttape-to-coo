## Summary

First Round Review interview with Raw Signal Group founders on why startup manager training fails and what works instead. Core thesis: first-time managers are promoted without feedback skills; the experience of being an untrained manager is stressful because they know they have feedback to give but don't know how to frame it without damaging the relationship. Training should focus on behavioural feedback habits (avoid pre-generalizing, mind-reading, commenting on motivations instead of behaviour) and practical rehearsal in real contexts rather than awkward role-play.

## Notable claims

- Untrained managers show up not knowing how to answer directs' questions or how to frame feedback without blowing up the relationship — when given actual skills, adoption is high ("water in the desert")
- People know they need to give regular feedback but it often doesn't go well; common failure habits include pre-generalizing, mind-reading, and giving feedback about motivations instead of behaviour
- Management training should connect to the thorniest manager tasks — feedback — and help managers self-assess whether feedback is useful
- Role-play with imaginary humans often fails to transfer to real difficult conversations; programs need to address what makes real feedback hard
