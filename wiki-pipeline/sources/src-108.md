## Summary

Meta-analysis of 18 studies (~2,500 participants) on workplace coaching outcomes. Finds consistent positive effects across performance/skills, well-being, coping, work attitudes, and goal-directed self-regulation. Supports coaching as a plausible intervention channel when the ask is founder behaviour change, not only process templates.

## Notable claims

- Coaching shows positive effects on performance/skills and goal-directed self-regulation
- Effects also appear on well-being, coping, and work attitudes
- Evidence base is moderate-to-strong at meta-analytic level — coaching is not placebo-only
- Coaching is one validated lever among others (role design, decision rights), not a substitute for structural change
