## Summary

Operator-turned-board-member essay (subtitle: "Don't let analysis get in the way of understanding your business") on how oversized board decks substitute the appearance of rigor for actual understanding. Written from the board-member side rather than founder or VC side, which gives it a distinct vantage point on what boards actually need to do their job well. Concrete, practical guidance on timing and precision standards.

## Notable claims

- Board materials should go out to the board at least three days ahead, and preferably seven — later delivery turns the meeting into first-read time instead of discussion time
- Financial statements in the board pack should aim for roughly 90% accuracy rather than a false-precision 100% — perfectionism on numbers delays the pack and doesn't change the decisions
- A very long, exhaustive board deck signals effort but often substitutes for genuine understanding of what's actually driving the business
- The point of board materials is to get the board to a shared, accurate picture of the business fast, not to demonstrate how much work went into the deck
