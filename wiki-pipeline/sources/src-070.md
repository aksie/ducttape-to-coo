## Summary

Structured English contribution from a Dutch practitioner interview (serial CEO as expert, Stefan Verkerk interviewing). Deepest experience at **~70 headcount**; formative patterns at **~20–40**. Multi-sided platform context (content × monetization × marketing). Organisation and personal names anonymised in the structured file; raw transcript in private team repo.

Covers **1.4 Who Decides What**, **1.1 Strategy & Planning** (planning cycle, not strategy craft), **3.2 Recruitment & Hiring**, and partial **1.3 Communication Norms**. Strong overlap/addendum with existing **1.4 @ first-hires** (src-063) and **1.4 @ growth** (src-064) — adds scale-specific mechanics: traffic control, Type 1/2 + integrative objection, CEO/CFO hat split, COO planning ownership, three-lens scored hiring.

## Notable claims

- Accountability without authority is the root failure — if someone is responsible for an outcome, they must be able to decide on it.
- At ~40+ on a multi-sided platform, a neutral “traffic control” role above functions is often missing when teams split.
- Type 1 (reversible) → delegate to role-owner; Type 2 (irreversible/cross-cutting) → integrative objection with reasoned block, not “I don’t like it.”
- Prefer “I’ve been heard” over harsh disagree-and-commit; CEO can still decide and ask for execution commitment.
- CEO and CFO (or P vs Q) are structurally opposed roles — don’t collapse into one person.
- Missions not titles; interim roles; accept org design revisits every few months.
- Planning: ~3 year priorities, not-list, quarterly 3–5 resourced projects, weekly issue-based MT review; COO owns process, CEO owns story.
- Hiring: hiring manager decides; manager+1 scores potential; culture checker scores values — no veto.
- Holacracy/PRINCE2: principles useful, full systems become theatre for ~90% of people.
