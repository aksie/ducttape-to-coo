## Summary

Early-stage investor argues most pre-seed/seed companies don't have a formal board with outside directors, but the best founders run quarterly "Not a Board Meeting" (NBM) sessions anyway — a deliberately more structured, higher-stakes checkpoint than a weekly or biweekly investor update call. Central claim: founders who build this operating rhythm before they're required to end up dramatically better prepared once a real board is seated, because they've already practiced synthesizing complex information, running a focused discussion, and using investors as a strategic resource rather than an audience to perform for.

## Notable claims

- Most pre-seed companies lack a formal board with outside directors, but that's not a reason to skip board-meeting discipline
- Founders who run structured quarterly "not-a-board-meeting" checkpoints before a formal board exists are measurably more prepared when one is seated later
- A quarterly structured checkpoint accomplishes things a standing weekly/biweekly investor update call cannot: forced clarity on what matters and mutual accountability
- The skill built in these informal sessions — synthesizing the business into a focused narrative, running a real discussion instead of a status readout — is the same skill formal board meetings require; better to build it early and cheaply
