## Summary

Hacker News discussion thread (2023 resurfacing of a 2021 post) on Jerry Neumann's essay arguing that VC-backed boards are far more likely to fire a founder-CEO than the sanitized "board advice" genre implies. Thread mixes founder and investor commenters reacting to the essay's central claim: the decision to stop being CEO is often the board's, not the founder's, and CEO transitions usually range from pressured resignation to being escorted out, with the founder choosing their own successor being the rare, good-case outcome. Note: this record is built from search-engine-indexed summaries of the thread rather than a full fetch (news.ycombinator.com is not reachable from this environment) — treat individual comment attributions as approximate.

## Notable claims

- Board advice content aimed at founders is systematically upbeat; the lived reality of board-founder power dynamics is much harsher
- Three realistic outcomes when a board loses confidence in a CEO: founder proactively finds their own replacement (rare/good), founder is told to resign or be fired, founder is removed abruptly — the first outcome is the exception, not the norm
- Commenters converge on the idea that founders who treat "the board works for me" as literally true are miscalibrated about where formal power sits once outside investors hold board seats
- Thread reinforces that board control is a function of cap table and bylaws, not goodwill — founders who don't understand this are surprised later
