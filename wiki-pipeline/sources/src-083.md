## Summary

HBR Classic (originally 1972; republished May–June 1998 with Greiner commentary). Argues organisations grow through alternating periods of *evolution* (steady growth under one management pattern) and *revolution* (crisis that forces a new pattern). Five evolutionary phases — Creativity, Direction, Delegation, Coordination, Collaboration — each ends in a characteristic crisis. The irony: the solution that unlocks one phase becomes the problem that forces the next. Managers who cling to past practices stall or fail.

## Notable claims

- Managerial problems and practices are rooted in time and size; they do not last the whole life of an organisation
- Each phase is both result of the previous phase and cause of the next
- Phase 1 Creativity: founders are product/market focused and often disdain management; ends in a *leadership crisis* when informal methods break
- Founders often resist stepping aside or changing style even when temperamentally unsuited to the next job
- Phase 2 Direction: functional structure, budgets, formal hierarchy; ends in *autonomy crisis* when lower levels need decision rights top will not give
- Phase 3 Delegation → *control crisis*; Phase 4 Coordination → *red-tape crisis*; Phase 5 Collaboration
- A major solution in one period becomes a major problem in a later period
