## Summary

Les McKeown (*Predictable Success*, 2010) describes Whitewater as the third growth stage after Early Struggle and Fun: complexity from successful growth produces errors, emergencies, and loss of the old "everything just worked" feel. Escape requires installing systems while still growing, and adding a Processor (then Synergist) to a team that previously only needed Visionary + Operator. Related post on executive-team imbalances: V/O is right for Fun and becomes the blocker in late Fun / early Whitewater; Visionaries and Operators instinctively resist process.

## Notable claims

- Whitewater arrives as a natural outgrowth of Fun's success; main cause is complexity from that growth
- Leadership may take up to a year to recognise Whitewater — at first it looks like bumps in the road
- Being in Whitewater is not a choice; getting out of it is
- Focus shifts from growth-at-any-cost toward efficiency and effectiveness
- Four leadership styles: Visionary, Operator, Processor, Synergist (VOPS)
- Fun needs Visionary + Operator; too much process early slows growth
- Whitewater needs the Processor (systems, policies, repeatability); Processors too early slow Fun
- Synergist needed later to reach Predictable Success; too early also holds you back
- V/O teams in late Fun / early Whitewater drop balls because they lack capability to handle creeping complexity
- Choice: return permanently to Fun (grow but never scale) or push through by adding heavy Processor then Synergist
- Related: Predictable Success is growth plus systems; later decline path includes Treadmill → Big Rut → Death Rattle when process overshoots
