## Summary

YC Continuity essay (2016; also in YC Library). Startup CEOs move through three phases: (1) build a product users love as Doer-in-Chief; (2) build a company to capture demand as Company-Builder-in-Chief (~20–25 to ~400–500 people); (3) harvest profits to fund new products. Phase 2 requires stopping Phase 1 doing so only-CEO work gets time: hire/coach a leadership team, create Mission–Strategy–Metrics alignment, and nurture culture (co-authored, not solo-authored). Success signal: substantial unscheduled think time. Text provided by curator 2026-09-16 after old blog.ycombinator.com URL 404'd.

## Notable claims

- Phase 1 CEO = Doer-in-Chief; delegation should not be in your vocabulary until demand outruns the small team
- Phase 2 (~20–25 people start): stop coding, demos, support, menial "no one's job" tasks — lead more by doing less
- Transition confuses the team (what does the CEO do now?) — still necessary
- Replace yourself by hiring people better than you into leadership; even Product can be delegated day-to-day
- Only-CEO tasks: (1) hire senior leaders and make them work well together; (2) Mission / Strategy / Metrics; (3) culture (shared authorship)
- Manage executives to quarterly/annual outputs, not weekly milestones
- Expect ≥25% of leadership hires not to work out; fire too slowly costs more credibility than a void
- Up to ~50% of CEO time on hiring/managing senior team is normal and well spent
- Write Mission-to-Metrics in long form; over-communicate until people decide well in your absence
- Culture: codify authentic past behaviour with early employees; don't invent values in a quiet room alone
- Measure of Phase 2 success: ~50% unscheduled time to think and stay ahead (Jobs via Pixar anecdote)
