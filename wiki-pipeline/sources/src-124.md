## Summary

LinkedIn post by a partner at a fund, captured 29 September 2026 from the short link above. The post describes the founder's job in three revenue bands on the way to a large company. It is one person's framework. The bands are revenue, not headcount, so they do not line up with the wiki's stages. Famous-company examples in the post (a visionary founder paired with an operational partner, and one person who did both) are illustrations, not a hiring recipe.

Numbering starts at src-124 so it does not collide with src-117 on the commercial-operator branch or src-118–123 on the first-hires satisfaction branch.

## The three bands, paraphrased

- Up to about $1 million: the founder is the person who does the work. Often no product, no customers, and no team. Revenue moves through the founder's own selling and building.
- About $1 million to $50 million: the founder becomes a player-coach. Hire people, teach them the work you did, and manage them until they do it better. Later, hire executives who can run a function and tell the company story without the founder in the room. The skills are hiring and coaching.
- About $50 million to $500 million: two jobs remain once executives run the day. One carries the story so it still works when people who have never met the founder repeat it. The other turns finance into operations: what will move the business, and how that becomes a result. If the founder is not excellent at the second job, hire a partner for it. Hiring someone to carry the story in the founder's place is much harder.
