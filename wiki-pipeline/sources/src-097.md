## Summary

Founder-side account of preparing for a company's first board meeting, written from direct first-person experience rather than as general advice. Describes a concrete prep timeline (outline three weeks out, draft content two weeks out, finalized deck one week out, pre-read shared the Friday before the meeting) and includes an outside quote from another startup CEO (Lindsay Tjepkema of Casted) reminding first-time founders that the board meeting is fundamentally still their meeting to run, not a performance to survive. Useful as a founder-side counterweight to the several investor-authored sources in this corpus.

## Notable claims

- A workable first-board-meeting prep timeline: rough outline three weeks out, draft content two weeks out, finalized materials one week out, pre-read circulated the Friday before
- First-time founders are prone to feeling intimidated or star-struck by board members with bigger reputations or more experience — the corrective is remembering it is still the founder's meeting to run
- Never surprise the board in the meeting itself — anything material should already be known to them before they walk in
- Presenting to the board using the same style and depth used for internal company updates does not work — board communication needs a deliberate reset, not a copy-paste of internal reporting
