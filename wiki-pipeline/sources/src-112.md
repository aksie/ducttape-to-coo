## Summary

Second interview with a partner at the same impact-VC fund as the earlier board-founder-roles research (src-087–097) and the shareholder-reporting series. This partner has an engineering/consulting background (Delft, McKinsey), joined the fund pre-financial-crisis, and covers energy/impact sectors. The fund has independently built its own internal maturity framework (a "compass" of impact/mission/culture, three phases — Pioneer/Organizing/Management — mapped across strategy/people/operations/finance, i.e. a Rockefeller-Habits-style wheel), used informally across their portfolio.

Topics covered: how the fund allocates its own time across portfolio companies (mostly people, some finance quality-control, light-touch strategy); a board-pack anecdote about OKR-definition theater without an actual key result or owner; board composition philosophy (small board, 1-2 external seats, rotating the fund's own seat to a trusted industry expert after 1-2 years); the mechanics and limits of board leverage over an underperforming company (strongest at financing, weak afterward); a failed COO placement (role reshaped around a likeable candidate instead of the defined need); the three recurring scale-phase founder shortfalls the partner sees across the portfolio; an explicit rebuttal (unprompted, in response to a leading question) of the idea that impact-fund reporting is any "softer" or more story-shaped than cash reporting; and a strategy-communication-frequency anecdote ("told it 52 times, a team of 60 each heard it once").
