## Summary

Article by Rachel Turner (author of *The Founder's Survival Guide*, 2022; VC Talent Lab) introducing the WAM model. Founders who succeed at start-up often keep applying the same leadership mode after the company scales; those strengths become the scale-up's kryptonite. Three modes map to stages: Brave Warrior (start-up), Considered Architect (scale-up), Wise Monarch (grown-up). Success requires flexing between modes for the challenge at hand, not a single default.

## Notable claims

- If you don't scale your leadership as you scale the company, you are unlikely to survive the scale-up journey
- Founders often assume "who I am is how I lead" and double down on what worked when it stops working
- Brave Warrior: single-minded, bulletproof self-belief, fast, full of ideas, comfortable with risk — right for start-up
- Considered Architect: team/systems thinker, calm, planner, systematiser — right for scale-up
- Wise Monarch: strategist, gravitas, politically astute, diplomat/figurehead — right for grown-up company
- Modes are not right/wrong in absolute terms; they are right or wrong for a specific stage or challenge
- Case pattern: visionary founder who hammers every problem personally, cancels plans/meetings, treats team pushback as "whinging" — strengths that raised capital become liabilities
- Related framing (book / client language): "killer shots" that were assets become liabilities when over-applied
