## Summary

Adizes' PAEI code (*The Ideal Executive*; also *Managing Corporate Lifecycles*) defines four necessary management roles: Producer (short-run effectiveness), Administrator (short-run efficiency / systematising), Entrepreneur (long-run effectiveness / vision and risk), Integrator (long-run efficiency / culture so no one is indispensable). No single person can excel at all four; missing roles create predictable mismanagement patterns. Roles develop in a sequence along the corporate lifecycle; on the typical Western path E then P dominate early (Go-Go = PaEi), and A is the hard third role to integrate because Entrepreneur energy resists Administration. Failure to bring A in successfully is the founder's trap.

## Notable claims

- Four roles — Produce, Administer, Entrepreneur, Integrate — are necessary and together sufficient for good management
- P: what should be done (short-run effective); A: how (short-run efficient); E: why/when (long-run effective); I: who (long-run efficient / organic)
- No single person can excel at all four; good managers need at least a modicum of each; teams must cover the set
- PAEI roles develop and decay in a predictable lifecycle sequence; current missing roles predict the next generation of problems
- Typical Western path: E first (Courtship), then P (Infant), then P+E together (Go-Go = PaEi), then A must enter (hard), then I
- In Go-Go, A and I are normally missing; if founders fire successive COOs / Administrators and never develop A and I, that becomes the pathological founder's trap
- E passionately resists A — experiences Administration as enemy even while needing it; without I, E and A destroy each other
- Organizations that keep all four roles strong and balanced can remain in Prime
- Primary books: *Corporate Lifecycles* / *Managing Corporate Lifecycles*; *The Ideal Executive*
