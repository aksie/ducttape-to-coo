## Summary

Ask HN thread where a solo founder asks how to retain control of their board as they bring on institutional investors. Commenters (a mix of founders and people describing themselves as having negotiated term sheets) converge on board composition and seat-appointment mechanics — who gets to appoint which seats, and how that's negotiated at each round — as the real lever, more so than dual-class stock, which is rare outside of very hot deals. Discussion emphasizes that "board dynamics are misunderstood by early stage founders" and that leverage to negotiate board composition comes from company performance, not from legal cleverness. ## Notable claims

- Board seat-appointment structure (who appoints which seats) is the practical lever for control, not dual-class stock, which is uncommon outside unusually competitive deals
- Dual-class / super-voting structures are frequently stripped out at the first or second institutional round even when founders start with them
- A founder's real bargaining power over board composition comes from company performance and traction, not from contract terms negotiated in advance
- Founders early in fundraising often misjudge how board dynamics will play out once outside directors are seated — the thread repeatedly corrects this assumption
