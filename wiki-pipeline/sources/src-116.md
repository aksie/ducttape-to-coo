## Summary

First Round Review case study of AltSchool's quarterly performance-review system adapted from Google for startup pace. Ventilla argues startups that stay "loosey-goosey" until a financing round often hit a wall at 3–5 years when professional managers are hired over star performers who never got the feedback they needed. AltSchool runs quarterly reviews with self-assessment, peer review, manager review, and a calibration committee; process scaled from ~25 to 160+ employees while keeping time cost roughly flat (~2 hours per employee per quarter).

## Notable claims

- Startups often hit the wall at 3–5 years: everything loosey-goosey, then financing + growth + need for professional managers; star performers hired over without prior feedback on management skills they could have developed
- Quarterly reviews matched startup pace better than annual ("doing annual performance reviews seems just useless" when things change constantly)
- Calibration committee reviews packets for consistency; quarterly reviews yield comp brackets and bonus parameters from ratings
- Promotions assessed against a rubric for the next level (e.g. % completion of Level-5 criteria while still Level-4) — documented, not ad hoc
- Without performance data and formal levels, compensation and advancement decisions are arbitrary at worst and hard to get right at best
