## Summary

HN discussion of when a startup needs a COO. Practitioners argue a COO’s job is discipline, structure, inter-department communication, and honest opposition to the CEO — not a generic “busy founder” relief valve. Not every company needs one; wild-west early stages can be intentional. Selecting the right person matters more than the title.

## Notable claims

- Hire a COO for process discipline and cross-team structure, not because the founder is overloaded
- COO should be able to challenge the CEO without becoming obstructive
- Early startups may rationally delay a COO while speed and informality still win
- Wrong COO hire is costly — role definition first, title second
