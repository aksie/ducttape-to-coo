## Summary

VC firm reference content urging founders to take board-meeting discipline seriously from day one rather than waiting for Series A/B. Central argument: who you put in the room matters more than almost anything else — map the specific areas where the business needs to excel, and make sure a board member exists who can speak with authority on each. Names a specific, named founder failure mode: using board time to justify the CEO's own performance to the board (self-defense mode) instead of extracting value from the people in the room, which the piece says can consume the majority of a meeting's time.

## Notable claims

- Board composition — who is actually in the room — matters more than almost any other factor in whether board meetings are useful
- Founders should map the specific capability areas the business needs to excel in and deliberately seat a board member who can speak with authority on each
- A common founder failure mode is using board meetings to justify their own performance and decisions to the board rather than using the time to extract help — this can consume the majority of meeting time
- Treating board-meeting discipline as a "wait until Series A/B" problem wastes years of compounding relationship- and communication-skill-building value
