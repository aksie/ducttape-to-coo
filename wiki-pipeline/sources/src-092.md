## Summary

VC partner's Forbes column on why startup boards commonly underperform their potential. Core reframe: founders often treat the board as a reporting forum (present results, take questions), when the real value of a board is forward-looking — decision support, problem-solving, and strategic input — not backward-looking reporting. Also flags board bloat: boards grow too large as founders offer a seat to every investor in a round, when roughly five members (an odd number, for decisive voting) is closer to the functional ideal.

## Notable claims

- Founders commonly misconceive the board as a forum for reporting past results rather than a forum for forward-looking decision support and problem-solving — this misconception is itself a root cause of low-value board meetings
- The real value a board should add is in planning and strategy, not in reviewing what already happened
- Boards commonly become oversized because founders offer every investor in a round a board seat; around five members, an odd number for decisive votes, is closer to the functional target
- When founders and directors don't understand these two points, boards not only fail to add value — in extreme cases they actively create additional problems for the company
