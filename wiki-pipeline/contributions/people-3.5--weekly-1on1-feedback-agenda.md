## Process placement

**Primary home: 3.5 Performance Management** — the logged feedback trail is what makes quarterly or annual check-ins a summary, not an ambush.

**Cross-link: 3.6 Employee Satisfaction & Feedback** — owns 1-on-1 cadence and pulse; this contribution is the *structure* inside each weekly 1-on-1.

## What does good look like?

Every weekly 1-on-1 runs from the same shared agenda doc. Standing topics repeat every week so nothing important depends on whoever remembers to bring it up. Direct reports add their own agenda points before the meeting. One standing slot is **Feedback** — both directions: what they want you to know, and what you want them to know.

## What should you actually do?

Use one doc (or recurring note) per report with a fixed weekly template. Before the meeting, the report adds items; you add yours. Always include a **Feedback** section. After the meeting, write down the feedback you actually shared — both what you gave them and what they gave you — in that doc. That log is what you refer back to when you prep a quarterly check-in or annual review; nothing in the formal conversation should be news.

## What breaks or goes wrong?

Without the written trail, managers reconstruct performance from memory at review time. People experience formal feedback as new even when you thought you said it months ago. The quarterly review becomes a debate about whether something was ever raised.

## How does this change as you grow?

At early revenue a shared doc per report is enough. Later you may move the same pattern into a lightweight HR tool or manager workspace — but the standing agenda + bidirectional feedback log should survive the tooling change.
