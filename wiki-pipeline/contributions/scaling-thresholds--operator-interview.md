# Practitioner extract — What changes when you grow past each threshold

**Scope:** Cross-cutting interview on how the operating model has to change as headcount grows, with explicit thresholds around **~40** and **~100** people. Opinion and pattern from one company. It is not framework canon.

**Cross-cutting thesis:** At every stage *something* is the constraint. Early on you find it, fix it, and move to the next one (Theory of Constraints). **Somewhere between ~40 and ~100 people, fixing single constraints stops being enough: the way of working itself has become the constraint**, and you have to change the system. Which system you switch to depends on your culture, sector and business model; there is no standard answer.

---

## 1. The system becomes the constraint (~40–100)

*Maps to **1.5 Organizational Design** (growth → scaled); touches **1.4 Who Decides What**.*

**What good looks like**
- Leadership keeps asking "what is the constraint right now?" and fixes it quickly, then looks for the next one. There is always a next one.
- Around ~40–100 people, leadership recognises that the *system* (span of control, how steering is organised) is the bottleneck. Adding one more fix won't help.

**What you actually need to do**
- Redesign how span of control and steering are organised when you approach this threshold. There is no template: the right model depends on culture. At this company it became a **cell model** (small, largely self-steering units). *[details of the cell model not discussed]*

**Signs things are broken** (what happens if you don't change the system)
- People running the primary process no longer do what leadership designed or intended. *(output_quality)*
- People start leaving out of frustration, or because signals from the floor no longer reach leadership. *(output_quality)*
- You can't onboard new customers because delivery capacity is full. *(process_entry)*
- In short: growth stalls, and the symptoms can show up anywhere.

---

## 2. Decisions (and their "why") are how culture transfers; watch copying errors

*Maps to **1.4 Who Decides What** and **3.5 Performance Management** (growth → scaled); touches **1.5**.*

**What good looks like**
- Leaders explain the **why** behind decisions, even briefly: "we don't mind losing money on this, because this client matters and customer satisfaction comes first." That reasoning is the culture. It's what people copy.
- The next layer of leaders emerges from the people who watched those decisions up close.

**What you actually need to do**
- When next-layer leaders start deciding "the same way," check how they're doing it. Expect **copying errors** (small distortions of the original reasoning). *[transcript: "zelfdeilingsfoutjes", likely "copy/translation errors"]*
- **Correct a wrong call immediately**, even when growth pressure makes it tempting to let it slide.

**Signs things are broken**
- Mistakes by next-layer leaders go uncorrected "for the sake of growth". The practitioner calls this a structural flaw (*weeffout*) with a **double cost**: (1) the company is steered wrongly, and (2) everyone learns that mistakes here don't matter much, so you can stay put. *(output_quality)*

**Context note:** This company skipped the ~10–25 phase (it was ~17 people when the practitioner joined) and was fairly top-heavy, so a lot could be concentrated at the top. The copying-error pattern was observed "very clearly" in the later growth phase.

---

## 3. Keep early-tenure training in-house

*Maps to **3.3 Onboarding** and **3.7 Learning & Development** (growth → scaled).*

**What good looks like**
- Training is delivered largely by your own people: subject-matter experts for content, internal people for (professional) skills, and outside help added selectively.
- **The first ~2 months of a new hire's tenure stay in-house**, because that's when culture is transferred.

**Signs things are broken**
- As the company grew, someone decided to outsource training, including the critical first two months. The company "no longer produced its own leadership," and a lot of culture was lost. They later reversed it and dropped the provider, but **repairing culture costs much more than keeping it**. *(output_quality)*
