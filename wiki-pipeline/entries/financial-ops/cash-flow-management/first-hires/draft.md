# Cash Flow Management — First Hires (2–10 people)

> At first hires, cash flow is **payroll timing plus runway visibility** — a Cash tab in the same sheet as your plan, updated monthly (weekly when tight). Not a treasury department; enough math to know how many months you have before the next hire or payroll shock.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **A Cash tab in the same Google Sheet as [2.2 Financial Planning](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md)** — opening balance, monthly burn, runway in months — kept aligned with cumulative cash on the main P&L tab so the numbers cannot drift apart.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **A 3–6 month forward cash view** — enough to see the next payroll cycles and one hire's cash impact, not a rolling 13-week treasury model.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
- **Runway known to the nearest month**, with a **named response when it drops below six months** and there is no credible path back within 90 days — fundraise prep, cuts, or facility use from your [2.2 trigger list](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md), not ad-hoc panic.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
- **Survival math assumes zero revenue** — you do not spend as if projected inflows are already in the bank.
  <!-- sources: src-068 (First Round / Rob Hayes, vc_portfolio_interest) | triangulated: src-065 downside rows in 2.2 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
- **Cash position is visible beyond the CEO** — scorecard, open books, or a short monthly review — so burn is a shared reality, not a founder secret.
  <!-- sources: src-073 (HN), src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
1. **Build the Cash tab** in your planning sheet: opening balance → monthly burn → runway in months. Tie opening balance and burn to cumulative cash on the main tab; headcount and triggers live in [2.2](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md).
   <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
2. **Calculate true monthly burn before any hire** — all cash out each month: payroll fully loaded, contractors, software, marketing, taxes, insurance, and operational spend. Salary alone is not burn.
   <!-- sources: src-067 (practitioner blog) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
3. **Before every hire, run a runway before/after table** — cash on hand, monthly burn, runway in months — and update the Cash tab within a working day. If you cannot answer "what happens to cash if they start next month?", the offer waits.
   <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution), src-067 (practitioner blog) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
4. **Update the Cash tab monthly** at minimum; **review cash weekly when runway is under 18 months** or cash is lumpy (payroll, tax, large invoices).
   <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution), src-005 (HN) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
5. **Map payroll timing with your accountant** — when salary, employer costs, and tax actually leave the bank — so the forecast shows when cash moves, not accrual fiction. NL payroll detail: see [3.1](../people/3.1--first-hires.md) / [4.5](../legal/4.5--first-hires.md).
   <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
6. **When runway drops below six months with no path back in 90 days**, execute the pre-agreed response from your [2.2 trigger → action list](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md) — do not wait for a crisis meeting.
   <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-012 -->
7. **Share cash in bank, burn, and runway with the team** at ~7–10 people. Early employees took risk; hiding the numbers erodes trust and hides problems others could spot.
   <!-- sources: src-073 (HN) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-013 -->
- **You don't know burn rate to within ~20%.** You are approving hires and spend without a reliable cash picture — bank balance alone is misleading once payroll runs.
  <!-- sources: src-005 (HN) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-014 -->
- **Bank or venture debt line counted in runway.** The facility hides how fast you burn through both pools — plan as if undrawn debt does not exist.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution), src-068 (First Round / Rob Hayes, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-015 -->
- **A hire signed without modeling runway before/after** — you only noticed months of runway disappeared after the offer.
  <!-- sources: src-067 (practitioner blog) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-016 -->
- **You can't answer "what happens to cash if we hire X next month?" within a working day.**
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-017 -->
- **A bad hire at ~12 months runway cost 2–3 months of founder distraction** before you cut — enough to jeopardize the company when runway is short.
  <!-- sources: src-072 (HN) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-018 -->
- **Only the CEO holds the cash numbers.** Everyone else optimizes locally while collective burn compounds.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution), src-073 (HN) -->

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-019 -->
- **You think you know burn but cannot state exact monthly cash out** — hiring decisions are being made in the dark.
  <!-- sources: src-067 (practitioner blog) -->

## How this evolves next

<!-- claim-id: c-020 -->
- **At Early Revenue, extend to a rolling 12-month cash forecast**, track against budget monthly, and use scenario planning for hire/spend decisions — coordinate with fundraising if runway tightens. The Cash tab habit scales; you are not replacing it with a separate tool.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

## Tools & resources

<!-- claim-id: c-021 -->
- **Same Google Sheet as [2.2 Financial Planning](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md)** — main P&L tab for cumulative cash, dedicated Cash tab for runway math.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

- **Accountant or bookkeeper** — loaded costs and payroll timing; founders own the cash review cadence and runway alerts.
