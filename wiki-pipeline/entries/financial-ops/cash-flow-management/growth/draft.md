# Cash Flow Management — Growth

> In a people business, growth hiring is a cash bet: you pay people long before they bill, and longer than clients take to pay. Someone has to own that gap before payroll is the thing that reveals it.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
- **After a cash scare, someone owns the plan, and you are less dependent on a bank to reach money you have already earned.** A lender that understands the business can reassure you in minutes. That is not the same as control of your own cash. The founders here decided they never wanted that feeling again, and financial ownership was formalised afterwards.
  <!-- sources: src-117 (commercial-operator, people_business) -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
1. **Before a hiring wave, write down how long a new person takes to become billable, and compare it with how fast clients pay.** Here that was roughly two months of training and about four months until billable, longer than the payment terms. Results were expected to follow from hiring, training and selling well. That bet held until the liquidity squeeze. The squeeze is the cash process.
   <!-- sources: src-117 (commercial-operator, people_business) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **Cash planning sits informally with one founder and becomes real only when payroll is at risk.** The credit facility is the first serious conversation. By then the gap has already been run.
  <!-- sources: src-117 (commercial-operator, people_business) -->
