# Employee Satisfaction & Feedback — First Hires (2–10 people)

> At this size you do not need a survey. Someone from leadership takes a walk or a lunch with each person once a month and asks how they actually are.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **Each person has a monthly walk or lunch with someone from leadership, and the time is theirs.** A walk or a coffee is enough. The project list stays in the week. This conversation is about them.
  <!-- sources: src-118 (stefanverkerk), src-119 (HN) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **You can say how each person is doing without a form.** With fewer than about ten people, a survey answer has a name. The conversation is the record.
  <!-- sources: src-118 (stefanverkerk), src-121 (Hackernoon, sells_manager_software) -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
1. **Put a monthly walk or lunch with each person on the calendar, and keep the date.** One leader can still do this with everyone at 2–10 people. The slot only works if it survives a busy week.
   <!-- sources: src-118 (stefanverkerk), src-119 (HN) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
2. **Ask what they like, what they do not like, how happy and engaged they feel, whether you are letting them use their full talent, and how burned out they are from 0 to 10.** People do not volunteer overload. A number is easier than a confession, and the talent question is how you hear that someone strong is bored before they leave.
   <!-- sources: src-118 (stefanverkerk), src-120 (Meiert) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
3. **Listen. Give advice only when they ask for it.** Arriving with your own list turns their time into your meeting. What they would not say in the group only comes out if you leave room for it.
   <!-- sources: src-119 (HN) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
4. **If something is wrong this week, say it this week.** The month is the right gap for "how are you, really." It is the wrong gap for a problem that is already costing them the week.
   <!-- sources: src-119 (HN) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
- **You ask how the project is going, and you never get to how they are.** Status repeats the standup and uses up the room the talent question needed.
  <!-- sources: src-119 (HN), src-120 (Meiert) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
- **You send an engagement survey to a team small enough that every answer has a name.** The score hides the person, and you do not yet have an HR loop to do anything with it. Sit with them instead.
  <!-- sources: src-118 (stefanverkerk), src-121 (Hackernoon, sells_manager_software) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
- **The monthly round has become ceremony.** Everyone still talks all day, and the lunch produces nothing you did not already hear in the hallway. Keep it short while that is true. The lunch is doing its job when a worry no longer arrives without an appointment.
  <!-- sources: src-123 (HN) -->

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
- **You have booked a weekly sit-down with everyone, and the week is full of meetings.** Around ten people that load is real. A monthly deeper conversation is the first-hires rhythm. Weekly belongs to the next stage.
  <!-- sources: src-119 (HN) -->

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
- **You cancel the lunch when the week gets busy.** A skipped conversation tells them they matter less than the fire, and they stop bringing the real thing.
  <!-- sources: src-119 (HN) -->

## How this evolves next

<!-- claim-id: c-012 -->
- **When you can no longer have this conversation with everyone yourself, the monthly walk stops being the system.** Each manager takes a more frequent one-to-one and writes down what was said. A survey starts to mean something only once a comment is no longer a name.
  <!-- sources: src-119 (HN) -->
