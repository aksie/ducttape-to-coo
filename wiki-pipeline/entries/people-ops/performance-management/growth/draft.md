# Performance Management — Growth (~26–50 people)

> At growth, performance management stops being "the founder plus a spreadsheet" and becomes an actual HR function — formal cycles tied to comp, a confidential person to go to, and managers who are trained to give feedback rather than trusted to improvise it.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **Every employee has a confidential person to go to inside HR — someone who is explicitly not their manager.**
  <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **HR process and policy are smooth and clearly defined for every step of the employee experience, run in-house** rather than improvised or outsourced.
  <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
- **You have a clear, explicit development and talent-retention strategy in place, including a defined path to promotion** — not just informal recognition of good work.
  <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
- **A formal review cycle (bi-annual or annual) is in place and tied to compensation, with promotion criteria documented** — not left as an unwritten judgment call each cycle.
  <!-- sources: src-115 (Molly Graham, sells_book), src-116 (First Round / AltSchool, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
1. **Train your managers explicitly on how to give feedback** — don't assume the skill transfers automatically from running weekly 1-on-1s at a smaller scale. Untrained managers default to avoiding hard conversations.
   <!-- sources: src-114 (First Round / Raw Signal, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
2. **Formally tie compensation reviews to performance-review outcomes, and document your promotion criteria** so advancement isn't a black box that people have to guess at.
   <!-- sources: src-115 (Molly Graham, sells_book), src-116 (First Round / AltSchool, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
3. **Bring in dedicated HR help in this headcount range** — the informal, founder-plus-spreadsheet system breaks under legal and calibration load before you reach 50 people; treat 35–50 as when it becomes mandatory, not optional. See [3.1 HR Administration](3.1--early-revenue.md) for when to make the hire.
   <!-- sources: src-076 (hacker_news) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
- **Managers avoid hard conversations, letting performance issues fester** — the most common failure mode once review cycles are formal but managers aren't trained to run them.
  <!-- sources: src-114 (First Round / Raw Signal, vc_portfolio_interest), src-077 (hacker_news) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
- **Negative performance feedback still appears for the first time in a formal review** — now with multiple managers running reviews instead of just the founder, this is a sign a manager individually avoided hard conversations for months, not just a company-wide gap.
  <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news) -->

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
- **Top performers' pay drifts below market because nobody formalized recognition** — they'll leave and you'll be shocked. Formal comp reviews (c-006) exist specifically to force this conversation before it becomes a resignation.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com, atom-617) -->

## Tools & resources

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
- **Situation–Behaviour–Impact (SBI) script for hard feedback** — specific moment, observable action, real consequence; strips emotional labels from corrective conversations. Still the right technique at this scale, now used by trained managers rather than just the founder.
