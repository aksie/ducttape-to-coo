# Performance Management — Early Revenue (~11–25 people)

> Informal feedback stops scaling around fifteen people. Introduce a lightweight quarterly review rhythm, connect role goals to company OKRs, and make sure nothing in a formal check-in is news — that work happens in [3.6 1-on-1s](3.6--early-revenue.md) all quarter.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **You run quarterly objectives alongside regular manager 1-on-1s** — OKRs or team goals set expectations and alignment; they are not a substitute for talking, and they are not a final performance rating by themselves.
  <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news), src-010 (firstround_review, Angus Davis via atom-027) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **Feedback flows in real time between formal review cycles** — quarterly check-ins summarize what people already know, not surprises. That requires goals, continuous feedback, and lightweight reviews working together — and weekly [3.6 1-on-1s](3.6--early-revenue.md) that log what was actually said.
  <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news), src-079 (human_experience), src-080 (openorg.fyi, atom-604) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
- **Scheduled performance conversations and written notes replace hallway-only feedback** — above ~15 people, informal osmosis breaks down; managers capture outcomes and follow up.
  <!-- sources: src-076 (hacker_news) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
- **Monthly career coaching is separate from weekly 1-on-1s** — status and blockers in the short meeting; growth, skills, and performance development in a dedicated monthly slot.
  <!-- sources: src-078 (firstround_review) | flags: vc_portfolio_interest -->

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
- **Individual goals connect to [company OKRs](../strategic/1.1--early-revenue.md) but OKR scores are decoupled from compensation and formal ratings** — tying them together invites sandbagging and kills ambitious goals.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review, Angus Davis), atom-027 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
- **Each role has a short written picture of what "doing well" looks like** — before review season, not invented in the review meeting. Subjective excellence is fine at this size; ambiguity about expectations is not.
  <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news), processes.json 3.5 stageFocus -->

<!-- claim-id: c-020 -->
- **Weekly 1-on-1s run from a shared standing agenda with a bidirectional Feedback slot** — same template every week; reports add their items; one fixed section covers feedback from them to you and from you to them.
  <!-- sources: src-079 (human_experience, stefanverkerk) | flags: practitioner_first_person -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
1. **Align quarterly personal, team, and company objectives** to your [1.1 planning](../strategic/1.1--early-revenue.md) rhythm — and keep [3.6 1-on-1s](3.6--early-revenue.md) for continuous feedback, not status-only standups disguised as management.
   <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news), src-076 (hacker_news) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
2. **Publish role expectations and informal performance levels before review season** — one page per function on what "meets" vs "exceeds" looks like, plus three or four simple levels (e.g. learning → solid contributor → senior) so reviews aren't invented in the room.
   <!-- sources: processes.json 3.5 stageFocus, src-077 (hacker_news), src-081 (hoffdigital.com, atom-610) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
3. **Run a quarterly check-in (~45–60 minutes) with prep on both sides** — employee self-reflection on wins, challenges, and next-quarter focus; manager prep from 1-on-1 notes and evidence (not memory); optional continue/stop/start or peer input. Schedule everyone's review within a two-week window. Not a ten-page form.
   <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news), src-075 (hacker_news), src-080 (openorg.fyi, atom-605), src-081 (hoffdigital.com, atom-611, atom-613) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
4. **Each report maintains a simple shared growth-goals doc**, updated before monthly career check-ins — wins, development areas, and next focus. A spreadsheet or doc beats an HRIS module at this headcount.
   <!-- sources: src-078 (firstround_review) | flags: vc_portfolio_interest -->

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
5. **Schedule the first formal performance conversation ~nine months after join, then at least yearly** — don't wait a full year before the first documented check-in or salary discussion feels arbitrary.
   <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-012 -->
6. **Managers prep each review with examples and follow-through** — not a perfunctory pass through corporate forms. If you cannot cite specific behavior and outcomes, postpone the conversation until you can.
  <!-- sources: src-078 (firstround_review) | flags: vc_portfolio_interest -->

<!-- claim-id: c-021 -->
7. **Log feedback in the 1-on-1 doc after every weekly meeting** — one shared note per report; write what you told them and what they told you. Reference it when you prep quarterly check-ins; follow up on growth commitments within three months.
  <!-- sources: src-079 (human_experience), src-081 (hoffdigital.com, atom-615), src-080 (openorg.fyi, atom-607) | flags: practitioner_first_person -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-013 -->
- **Negative performance feedback appears for the first time in an annual review** — the manager avoided hard conversations for months; the employee experiences it as an ambush, not development.
  <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news) -->

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-014 -->
- **Career progression and corrective feedback only happen in a once- or twice-yearly review** — you cannot wait that long for those conversations; the strongest tool is still the weekly or bi-weekly 1-on-1.
  <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news) -->

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-015 -->
- **You rolled out a heavy review template before you have a rhythm of talking, noting outcomes, and following up** — the form exists; nobody uses it consistently.
  <!-- sources: src-076 (hacker_news) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-022 -->
- **Formal review feedback was never written down in weekly 1-on-1 notes** — you reconstruct from memory; the report disputes whether you ever raised it. A log beats an argument about what was said months ago.
  <!-- sources: src-079 (human_experience, stefanverkerk) | flags: practitioner_first_person -->

<!-- claim-id: c-023 -->
- **Feedback turns personal ("hard to work with") instead of behavioral** — without a review structure people get defensive and nothing changes; use specific examples and frameworks like SBI.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com, atom-616) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-024 -->
- **Top performers' pay drifts below market because nobody formalized recognition** — they'll leave and you'll be shocked; reviews should force explicit comp conversations.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com, atom-617) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-025 -->
- **You copied enterprise 360s or page-long rubrics at ~20 people** — checkbox culture without behavior change; keep the cycle self-review, manager assessment, and one conversation.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com, atom-618) -->

## How this evolves next

<!-- claim-id: c-016 -->
At **growth**, add a formal bi-annual or annual review cycle, manager training on feedback, compensation reviews tied to performance, and documented promotion criteria — see [3.5 at Growth](3.5--growth.md). Hire dedicated HR help around 35–50 people before the informal system breaks under legal and calibration load.
<!-- sources: src-076 (hacker_news), processes.json 3.5 stageFocus growth -->

## Tools & resources

<!-- claim-id: c-017 -->
- **Simple growth-goals spreadsheet per report** — [First Round career-goals template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/12Z8euSULYHGSKgJMR47QuK2cEyHm0B-6hAmanqtVqQ0/edit?usp=sharing) (linked from Laura Cordova essay); enough before you buy performance software.
  <!-- sources: src-078 (firstround_review) | flags: vc_portfolio_interest -->

<!-- claim-id: c-018 -->
- **Optional light peer input as needed through the year** — highlights contributions a manager-only view misses; not a mandatory enterprise 360 cycle.
  <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-019 -->
- **[How to Make OKRs Actually Work at Your Startup](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-make-okrs-actually-work-at-your-startup)** (Angus Davis) — why OKRs are a communication tool, not a performance-rating system; pairs with goal-setting in reviews.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-026 -->
- **Run the full process manually in Notion or Google Docs for one quarter** before you buy performance HRIS — prove the rhythm first, then automate what already works.
  <!-- sources: src-080 (openorg.fyi, atom-606) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-027 -->
- **Quarterly Sit-Down (QSD) one-page form** — [SignalFire template approach](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/quarterly-sit-down-qsd): both sides fill independently (~15 min), swap at the meeting without pre-sharing, discuss misalignment. Accomplishments, goals, strengths, dev areas, upward feedback.
  <!-- sources: src-082 (signalfire.com, atom-619, atom-620, atom-621) | flags: vc_portfolio_interest -->

<!-- claim-id: c-028 -->
- **Self-review prompts before each check-in** — proudest win, hardest surprise, where you want to grow, what support would help (~30 minutes). Reflection, not a resume.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com, atom-611) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-029 -->
- **Situation–Behaviour–Impact (SBI) script for hard feedback** — specific moment, observable action, real consequence; strips emotional labels from corrective conversations.
  <!-- sources: src-080 (openorg.fyi, atom-609) -->
