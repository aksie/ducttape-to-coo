# Recruitment & Hiring — Growth (~50–150 people)

> At growth stage you start hiring senior leaders and C-level roles. The budget → offer → contract chain from [early revenue](../../../wiki/processes/people/3.2--early-revenue.md) still applies. Split interviews into three lenses (fit, potential, culture), **score don't veto**, and let the hiring manager decide.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **Senior hires go through three separate conversations — and no single interviewer has a veto.** The hiring manager checks fit and chemistry. Someone one level up checks potential only (live case, whiteboard — not a final hire/no-hire). A long-tenure culture carrier checks values over coffee. Each gives a signal; none can kill the hire alone.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner), atom-388, atom-389 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **The hire happens when all three conversations spark yes — not when one person says "not a fit."** A binary culture veto from one interviewer is too subjective once you are hiring heads and executives at scale.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner), atom-389 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
- **Inputs are scores the hiring manager weighs — not a unanimity rule.** Example: stronger on culture, slightly less potential, fine for the role now — the hiring manager decides and owns the tradeoff.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
- **Culture non-negotiables are few and owned by the hiring manager** — not a blanket "no culture fit" anyone can invoke. The culture checker scores against explicit values dimensions (e.g. entrepreneurship 3/5, alignment 4/5); the hiring manager interprets what matters for this role.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
- **A new leader gets the role immediately** — real decisions in their style from day one, with advice in the first weeks. Not months of partial projects while you retain the final call.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
1. **Run the three conversations in order — with different jobs for each round.** (1) **Hiring manager** — role fit, hard criteria, chemistry: did this meeting give you energy? (2) **One level up** — **potential only**: unprepared problem, whiteboard, live case. Output is a potential estimate, not hire/no-hire — **no veto**. (3) **Long-tenure culture carrier** — life, work, values over coffee. Also **no solo veto**. The three sparks together decide.
   <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner), atom-388 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
2. **Make explicit that a 3/5 culture score is a weighting input, not a kill switch** — trust a culture-literate hiring manager to own the tradeoff; tell interviewers upfront their score advises, it doesn't veto.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
3. **On an executive hire, hand over domain ownership from day one** — stutter-step onboarding (small shadow projects for months) is often the hirer's failure, not the hiree's. See [1.4 at Growth](../../../wiki/processes/strategic/1.4--growth.md) for decision-rights handover.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-012 -->
4. **Accept founder disillusion as the company scales** — people hired at 50+ are a different breed than founder types; specialists (legal, compliance, finance) won't feel like clones and shouldn't have to.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
- **A culture or values interview ends in a binary "not a fit" with veto power.** One subjective no blocks senior hires that the hiring manager and executive team would otherwise make — and the process stops scaling with you.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner), atom-391 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
- **You expect a specialist hire (e.g. CRO, CFO, Head of CS) to be friends with every other department head.** The bar is manager chemistry and values alignment — not universal friendship across the leadership team.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner), atom-392 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-013 -->
- **Ambiguity over who the hiring manager is** — recruiters burn out chasing approvals; candidates drop when nobody clearly owns the decision.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-015 -->
- **"We'll figure out the VP scope after they join"** — four months of project hopping, then "not fast enough." Scope and decision rights must be clear before offer — not discovered in month three.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-016 -->
- **The hiring manager compromises on profile because the market is tight, then delivery gets blocked** — the system is blamed when the tradeoff was never owned explicitly. If you hire below the bar, the hiring manager must say so and accept the consequence.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-014 -->
- **Founders re-enter late with an effective veto** — "we called so-and-so, we really shouldn't" funnels every hire back to a founder who no longer has context. Parallel: a former approver still asked to sign off on decisions they no longer understand.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-017 -->
- **Founders structurally disillusioned as the company scales** — even the executives you need feel wrong because they aren't founder clones. If every senior hire feels like a misfit, the problem is often your expectations, not the candidates.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## How this evolves next

<!-- claim-id: c-018 -->
- **The founder must hire the first ~50–100; then the decision right has to genuinely move to managers** — or hiring stalls. At scaled stage the same three-lens pattern extends into professional recruiting infrastructure (ATS, pipelines) — see [3.2 at Scaled](../../../wiki/processes/people/3.2--scaled.md).
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## Tools & resources

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
- **9-box thinking (performance × potential × values)** — use as shared language across the three conversations, not as a bureaucracy layer. Gives hiring manager, senior reviewer, and culture carrier the same vocabulary without a single scorecard veto.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner), atom-390 -->
