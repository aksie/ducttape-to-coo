- Reviewed by: "aksie"

- Edited claim text: - **Two decision paths, and everyone knows which one they are on.** Decisions the team can walk back — a vendor, a campaign, a way of working — sit with the role owner inside their mandate: consult, decide, move. Decisions that are expensive to walk back, or that bind other functions to them, run a short shared process instead: everyone affected is heard, and a role owner can stop it only by naming a concrete way it breaks something they are accountable for — not "I don't like it." That last rule is worth borrowing from Holacracy, a complete self-management system with its own rulebook, without taking the rest of it.

- Reviewed by: "aksie"

- Edited claim text: 5. **When every team is locally right and the shared outcome still fails, ask who owns the whole thing.** On a multi-sided business — dev, marketing, and monetisation each owning a slice — don't ask one side to write the others' strategy, and don't bolt coordination onto a function lead as an extra hat. Sometimes the answer is a neutral owner sitting above the functions who is accountable for the combined result; practitioners call this "traffic control." It is not required for every company at this size.
