# Who Decides What — Early Revenue (~11–25 people)

> At early revenue you stop being the default approver on everything. Name a decision owner per area, write simple escalation thresholds, and make it explicit: founders give input — area owners decide.

**Five ideas hold at every stage. Early revenue only changes the mechanics:**

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
1. **Accountability and authority travel together.** Whoever owns an outcome can decide on it — otherwise the team waits, and you end up back inside decisions you delegated.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
2. **Consult, then decide — don't decide by committee, and power and responsibility travel together as well.** One named person owns the call and has the responsibility to gather input from the people affected and the people with relevant expertise first. Gathering input is not the same as needing everyone to agree — the named person then has the power to decide.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
3. **Decide who decides before you need it.** Rules written mid-conflict read as a power move; the same rules written in calm are just a map you look up.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
4. **A decision you can walk back deserves less process than one you can't.** Cheap to reverse: one owner, light consult, move. Expensive or impossible to reverse: more people, more time, an explicit close.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
5. **Formalise only when the informal version visibly breaks.** Process added ahead of the pain costs more than it saves.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
**Carried over unchanged from earlier stages:** the ABC decision map (domain owner decides after consulting / discuss then tie-break / unanimous), a named decider who has to consult before closing anything, and one visible list of what still needs founder OK — hiring, spend above €X, pivots — against what the owner of the work decides alone. The three buckets come from [Andres Perea's founders'-agreement guide at Ascent Incubator](https://ascentincubator.com/legal-administrative/founders-agreements-the-founder-prenup-that-prevents-cofounder-conflict-and-speeds-up-mvp-execution/); the version this wiki uses lives in [1.4 — First Hires](1.4--first-hires.md). Early revenue extends the same pattern from founders to area owners: product, engineering, sales/GTM, and ops.
<!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), Ascent ABC (Andres Perea) -->

## What good looks like at this stage

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
- **Every major area has a named decision owner and written escalation thresholds** — who decides spend up to €X, customer exceptions, and local tradeoffs without founder OK. Founders agree the thresholds in a founder sync; area leads operate inside them.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), processes.json 1.4 stageFocus -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
- **Founders give input in meetings; the area owner decides — and can decide against dissent.** A call should not re-open because a founder was not in the room; if the founder wasn't there, the owner still owns it after consulting the people affected. That only holds when the role carries real decision authority and not just the task — *"I hear you, but I'm deciding — it's my responsibility."* Task owners without authority create endless alignment loops.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), HSG seed article, src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-434 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
- **Decisions the area owner can walk back stay with the area owner** — they listen, consult, then decide in their own style. A local, course-correctable bet, like a reversible €30k spend inside one team, should not land on a founder's desk by default.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-437 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
- **Roles built to push against each other sit on different people** — at this stage that is usually whoever owns pricing and whoever owns volume marketing; see [1.4 at Growth](1.4--growth.md) for the executive version.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-442 -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
1. **Write one page: named owner per area + escalation thresholds** — product, engineering, sales/GTM, ops/finance. For each: what they decide alone, what hits the founder approval list, what needs cross-founder or exec alignment. Show the team the escalation subset; keep nuance among leaders.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), reStruggle ~15–25 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-012 -->
2. **Run meetings with founder input, area-owner decision** — in the room: gather dissent, then the area owner states the decision and who needs to know. Codify within 48h per [1.3 Communication Norms](1.3--first-hires.md).
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-013 -->
3. **Mark which decisions are cheap to reverse and which are hard to undo.** Put worked examples next to the thresholds: cheap to reverse — a local vendor, a pricing test; hard to undo — a multi-year contract, a company-wide price lock. The first kind gets one owner and a light consult; the second gets more people, more time, and an explicit close.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-014 -->
4. **Assign missions, not permanent identity, when standing up a new lead** — interim scope ("six-month mission to stand up sales ops") eases later role changes; title-as-identity blocks splits you will need at growth.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-443 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-015 -->
5. **Formalise when you add the page, not when you're already in conflict** — trigger: decisions get re-opened weekly, or two teams block each other on the same priority. That is the signal for named owners + thresholds, not a headcount rule alone — and what you add is that one page, not an imported rulebook: a full RACI matrix (a grid naming who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed for every task) costs more than they return before the room actually stops working.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), reStruggle ~15–25, src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-024 -->
6. **When you keep a decision, write down the criteria and let others poke holes.** That is how they learn to take the next ones. Criteria that live only in the founder's head keep you as sole decider forever. Pair with [1.5 Organizational Design](1.5--early-revenue.md) — giving away the job — so teaching criteria is not an excuse to keep every call.
   <!-- sources: src-098 (HN Ask: How/When to Delegate?) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-016 -->
- **Decisions get re-litigated because a founder wasn't in the meeting** — the team learns to wait for the founder who was absent instead of accepting the area owner's call.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), processes.json 1.4 stageFocus -->

<!-- claim-id: c-017 -->
- **"But it used to work — why doesn't it now?"** — teams split but nobody owns coordinated moves across them; platform or cross-team outcomes fall between cracks. Nostalgia for the one-room phase is a signal to name deciders, not to re-merge teams.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-445 -->

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-018 -->
- **Founder still has effective veto on every hire after naming a hiring manager** — candidates and recruiters lose context when every offer funnels back for a late culture veto. See [3.2 Recruitment & Hiring](../people/3.2--early-revenue.md) for the approval chain.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-448 -->

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-025 -->
- **You hire a COO or chief of staff because you are overloaded — without a mandate for structure and honest pushback.** Title without behaviour change leaves you overruling ops or dumping undifferentiated work. Change the decision rights first; then staff the role that holds them. See also [1.5 Organizational Design](1.5--early-revenue.md).
  <!-- sources: src-100 (HN Should You Hire A COO?) -->

## How this evolves next

<!-- claim-id: c-019 -->
- **At growth**, your executives sit in two teams at once — their own function and the company — and the decisions that are expensive to walk back start running a shared process, with written per-role rules for what counts as a valid block. See [1.4 at Growth](1.4--growth.md).
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->
