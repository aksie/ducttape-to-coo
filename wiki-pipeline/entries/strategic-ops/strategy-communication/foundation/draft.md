# Internal Communication about Strategy — Foundation (1–5 people)

> At this size, strategy communication *is* planning communication. Setting priorities lives in 1.1; this page is how those priorities stay shared.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **Everyone can name the top priorities for the quarter without checking a document.**
  Same bar as 1.1: if people cannot repeat the priorities, they were not communicated — a doc nobody opens does not count.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **Everybody understands why these are the priorities.**
  Not just the list — the reasoning. People who only hear "what" cannot judge tradeoffs when the founder is not in the room.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
- **When priorities change, you say so to the whole team and explain why.**
  Silent drift is the failure mode: one founder starts acting on a new view of the market while others still work the old list.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
- **The founder is not fielding daily "what should I work on?" questions.**
  If planning context lives only in the founder's head, every work-selection decision requires a conversation — that is a communication gap, not a motivation gap.
  <!-- sources: src-021 -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
1. **Make the 3–5 quarterly priorities impossible to miss.**
   Whiteboard, pinned Slack message, or single shared slide — pick one place people already look and keep it current. Priorities themselves are set in 1.1.
   <!-- sources: src-026 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
2. **Run a 15-minute weekly priorities check with the full team.**
   What did we get done? What's blocking us? Does our work still serve the quarterly priorities? Adjust if needed. No slides, no prep.
   <!-- sources: src-026 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
3. **With the founding team, discuss weekly or biweekly whether these are still the right priorities — and only act on other things once you've agreed a change.**
   Then tell the rest of the team the same day.
   <!-- sources: src-026 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
4. **After each quarter, write a frank note: what went differently than planned, and next quarter's priorities — and send it to someone.**
   Even if that someone is only yourself. Verbal-only commitments are easy to rewrite in hindsight.
   <!-- sources: src-026 -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
- **Ask anyone "what are we focused on right now?" and they hesitate.**
  Planning isn't working as communication yet.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
- **You tell investors or your network different priorities than the ones written down for the team.**
  Usually means you think priorities should have changed, but never made the change explicit inside the company.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

### Founder time

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
- **The founders constantly have to explain what people should work on.**
  The weekly check and the visible list are supposed to answer that.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-012 -->
- **People work on things that aren't in the priorities because they didn't know the list existed or what it meant.**
  Not rogue behaviour — a transmission failure.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->
