# Internal Communication about Strategy — First Hires (2–10 people)

> At first hires the strategy story still travels by repetition in a small room — not by cascade machinery. That includes quarterly goals and current priorities from 1.1.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **Founders still tell the strategy story often enough to each employee, so that the early team shares one mental model.**
  Repeated narrative at this scale replaces formal cascade machinery — osmosis works when everyone is still in the same conversation often enough.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
- **Everyone can name this quarter's top priorities (and the primary growth metric) without looking them up.**
  Goals that live only in a kickoff doc are not communicated. Same bar as 1.1.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
- **Weekly (or bi-weekly) team meetings include progress against those goals — not only task status.**
  What moved the priorities? What's blocking them? Where do we need help?
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
- **When priorities change mid-quarter, founders say so explicitly and explain why.**
  Silent founder drift is the communication failure mode at this size.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
1. **Keep current quarterly goals visible in one place the team already looks.**
   Pinned message, whiteboard, or one shared page — update it when the list changes. Setting the goals is 1.1; keeping them in sight is this process.
   <!-- sources: src-026 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
2. **In the regular team meeting, open with the priorities — then status.**
   Two or three minutes: are we still on the quarter's goals? Then blockers and work.
   <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
3. **Repeat the strategy story in the same forums where you talk goals** — hiring conversations, weekly sync, and the quarter kickoff — so "why" and "what now" stay one narrative.
   <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
4. **At quarter start, close the last quarter out loud before setting the next:** how did we do, why, what changes — then the new goals.
   A written frank close (even short) beats a verbal rewrite; share it with the team, not only founders.
   <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review), src-026 -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **Multi-founder teams explain direction slightly differently to their functions** — creating unspoken frustration and uncertainty before anyone names the misalignment.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
- **Nobody can name the top priorities without checking a document** — or each person names a different list.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
- **Internal comms is treated as separate from strategy and nobody owns transmission.**
  A practitioner view at this stage: internal comms is a part of the strategy process, not a separate parallel workstream — if nobody owns telling the story, cascade will fail when headcount grows.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-012 -->
- **Goals exist in a document written at quarter start and are never mentioned in weekly meetings.**
  A goal that isn't spoken regularly is a wish. The meeting agenda is the transmission channel.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

## How this evolves next

At early revenue and growth, the story must flow down on a planning rhythm — see strategy-communication/growth.
