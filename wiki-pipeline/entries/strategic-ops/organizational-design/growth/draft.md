# Organizational Design — Growth (~26–50 people)

> At growth, org design stops being about the founder giving away jobs and starts being about whether the structure itself — board, blueprint, bus-factor — can survive without any one person, including you.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **You build systems and owners, not yourself as the fire department.** By this headcount the pattern should be structural, not a founder habit you're still building — if you're still personally the one who gets pulled into ops and product fires, org design hasn't caught up with headcount.
  <!-- sources: src-099 (First Round / Corcos, vc_portfolio_interest), src-101 (HN founder-mode, why) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **A practical test still holds: you have unscheduled time to think and stay ahead.** A calendar still full of other people's work at this size means the leadership layer below you isn't actually running independently yet.
  <!-- sources: src-111 (Rowghani / YC, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
- **Your organization runs according to a documented operational blueprint, not tribal knowledge held by a few people — and your management team is perceived by employees, not just by itself, as genuinely good leaders.** Two separate failure modes get conflated without this: a team can believe it's leading well while actually running on undocumented tribal knowledge that only works because the same few people are still around.
  <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
- **The organization has little dependency on any one or a few individuals.** If one person leaving would still be a crisis, org design hasn't caught up with headcount yet.
  <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
1. **Explicitly ask whether your founding leadership team is fit to grow the company internationally** — don't assume the team and habits that built the company domestically will scale into new markets by default. Some early leaders will start underperforming or leaving around this size, not necessarily because they got worse, but because role requirements shifted faster than they adapted — treat it as a structural signature of this transition, not automatically a personal failure.
   <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
2. **Ask your employees directly, not just your management team, whether they experience the leadership as good leaders.** Self-assessment from the top is not a reliable substitute for that signal.
   <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
3. **Push your board toward a genuinely well-functioning supervisory structure at this stage: an independent chair, real diversity, and members who will actually challenge your thinking** — not just approve decisions you've already made.
   <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
4. **Treat "is the management team and organization ready for the next phase" as a question you revisit on a recurring basis**, not a one-off milestone you check once and move past.
   <!-- sources: src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
5. **Manage senior people to quarterly and annual outputs — not weekly milestones — and fire underperforming leadership hires faster than feels comfortable.** Expect roughly 25 percent of your first executive hires not to work out.
   <!-- sources: src-111 (Rowghani / YC, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
- **You never replace yourself with leaders better than you in each major domain** — so you stay Doer-in-Chief and slow everyone else down.
  <!-- sources: src-111 (Rowghani / YC, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
- **Every departure or leave still feels like a crisis.** At this size that should be even less true than it was at early revenue — if losing one person can still derail the org, the bus-factor problem hasn't been fixed.
  <!-- sources: src-110 (practitioner, human:stefanverkerk), src-113 (SHIFT Invest framework, vc_portfolio_interest) -->
