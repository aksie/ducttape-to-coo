# Process Documentation — Early Revenue (~11–25 people)

> Successful growth outruns how you used to work — documentation is how you install the **Administrator** function the company now needs. Visionaries and Operators will experience that as slowing them down; that resistance is normal, not a signal to skip it.

## What good looks like at this phase

<!-- claim-id: c-001 -->
- **The four organisational functions are covered across the team — not all in the founder.** In Adizes' [PAEI model](../../../blog/what-got-you-here-is-whats-hurting-you-now.html): **Producer** (delivers results), **Administrator** (systematises and controls), **Entrepreneur** (vision and change), **Integrator** (builds culture and connection). No one person excels at all four — the founder can stay strong on E and P while someone else owns the A work this page is about: written processes and keeping them current.
  <!-- sources: src-086 (Adizes PAEI, atom-637) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-002 -->
- **A Processor mandate exists — explicit ownership of systems and repeatability.** Someone (often an ops lead, chief of staff, or fractional COO) is named to build policies, templates, and written how-tos — not as a side project between fires, but as part of their job.
  <!-- sources: src-085 (McKeown VOPS, atom-635) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-003 -->
- **Documentation is a habit, not a one-off cleanup.** When a new recurring process appears, it gets a page in the wiki or Notion the same week — with a named owner on that page. People actually use the space to onboard and to settle "how do we…?" questions.
  <!-- sources: processes.json 1.7 stageFocus (early-revenue) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-004 -->
- **Critical processes are formally owned and written down.** The highest-friction workflows — deploy, customer issues, new hire setup, invoicing — have a one-pager or checklist someone other than the founder can follow without a oral handoff. Ownership is on paper, not just in practice.
  <!-- sources: processes.json 1.7 stageFocus (first-hires seed), src-085 (atom-635) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-005 -->
- **You review documentation quarterly.** Stale pages get updated or archived; processes that still live only in the founder's head get scheduled for capture next.
  <!-- sources: processes.json 1.7 stageFocus (early-revenue) -->

## What you actually need to do

<!-- claim-id: c-006 -->
1. **Add Processor capacity before Whitewater becomes permanent.** Hire, promote, or assign an ops lead whose job includes systems, templates, and repeatability — McKeown's escape from Whitewater is Visionary + Operator **plus** Processor, not more heroics from the founder.
   <!-- sources: src-085 (atom-635) | flags: sells_coaching on src-085 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-007 -->
2. **List the 3–5 processes that break most often in your company** — e.g. onboarding, deploy, support escalations, billing, hiring setup. Rank by "founder still involved" and "new hire asks about this." Document those first — not everything at once.
   <!-- sources: processes.json 1.7 stageFocus (first-hires seed) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-008 -->
3. **Capture one process per cycle.** Record a Loom or voice note while doing it; have Claude or a colleague turn it into a one-pager; name the owner; publish in wiki/Notion. Repeat monthly until the top five are off the founder's head.
   <!-- sources: src-085 (atom-635), processes.json 1.7 stageFocus -->

<!-- claim-id: c-009 -->
4. **Put an owner field on every doc page** and a "last reviewed" date. If nobody owns it, it will rot — orphan pages are worse than no pages.
   <!-- sources: processes.json 1.7 stageFocus (early-revenue) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-010 -->
5. **Run a 30-minute quarterly doc review** with the Processor owner and area leads: what's stale, what's still oral-only, what broke twice since last quarter, and **where the founder is still the primary process** when a written handoff and named owner would scale better.
   <!-- sources: processes.json 1.7 stageFocus (early-revenue) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

<!-- claim-id: c-011 -->
- **The same mistakes and emergencies keep repeating** — successful growth created complexity the old informal way cannot carry. It stopped feeling like Fun; leadership may still dismiss it as "a rough quarter" instead of Whitewater. At scale, attrition and temporary coverage holes are a *constant*, not a one-off — if every departure feels like a crisis, the docs and ownership never caught up.
  <!-- sources: src-085 (atom-634), src-110 (atom-692) | flags: sells_coaching on src-085 -->

<!-- claim-id: c-012 -->
- **Processes break when one person is out — and someone is always out** — the how-to lived in their head or in DMs, not in a doc the team can find. Treat leave, sickness, and quits as the normal operating condition to design for, not as exceptions.
  <!-- sources: processes.json 1.7 stageFocus, src-086 (atom-637), src-110 (atom-692) -->

### Founder / key-person time

<!-- claim-id: c-013 -->
- **The founder doubles down on start-up strengths** — grabbing underperforming work, skipping management rhythm, treating every problem as hands-on — when scale-up friction needed structure and delegation instead. Often that shows up as **holding onto the primary process** (the sales motion, the product call, the support escalation) because letting go feels like losing the edge — documentation and a named owner are how that work scales without the founder in every loop.
  <!-- sources: src-084 (Turner, atom-632), src-086 (atom-637) | flags: sells_coaching -->

<!-- claim-id: c-014 -->
- **Documentation feels boring, bureaucratic, or "not us"** — and the founder quietly blocks or ignores it. That usually means the missing Administrator function threatens the identity that early success built on E and P.
  <!-- sources: src-086 (Adizes, atom-638) | flags: sells_coaching -->

### Process entry

<!-- claim-id: c-015 -->
- **New hires ask basic "how do I…?" questions** and get different answers from different people — or "ask Sarah" because nothing is written. Your wiki or Notion has not been updated in months.
  <!-- sources: processes.json 1.7 stageFocus (early-revenue), src-085 (atom-634) -->

## How this evolves next

<!-- claim-id: c-016 -->
- **At Growth (~26–50), informal docs become standards.** Templates, review cycles, findability rules, and "new hires self-serve most questions" — the habits you build here become the documentation system there. Expect today's fix to sow tomorrow's crisis if you over-document too early or never document at all; Greiner's irony applies to your wiki too.
  <!-- sources: src-083 (atom-630), processes.json 1.7 stageFocus (growth) -->

<!-- claim-id: c-017 -->
- **After Processor comes Synergist.** Once systems exist, you need someone who translates between Visionary, Operator, and Processor so the team stops treating process as the enemy of speed — that role lands in Predictable Success, not on day one of Whitewater.
  <!-- sources: src-085 (atom-635) | flags: sells_coaching -->
