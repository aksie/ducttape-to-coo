# Cash Flow Management — First Hires

> At first hires, cash flow is **payroll timing plus runway visibility** — Cash rows in the same sheet as your plan, updated monthly (weekly when tight). Not a treasury department; enough math to know how many months you have before the next hire or payroll shock.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **A set of rows about Cash in the same Google Sheet as [2.2 Financial Planning](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md).**
  Opening balance, monthly burn, runway in months — kept aligned with cumulative cash on the main P&L tab so the numbers cannot drift apart.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

- **Runway known to the nearest month**, with a **named response when it drops below six months** and there is no credible path back within 90 days — fundraise prep, cuts, or facility use from your [2.2 trigger list](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md), not ad-hoc panic.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

- **Survival math assumes zero new revenue.**
  You do not spend as if projected inflows are already in the bank.
  <!-- sources: src-068 (First Round / Rob Hayes, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

- **Cash position is visible beyond the CEO** — scorecard, open books, or a short monthly review — so burn is a shared reality, not a founder secret.
  <!-- sources: src-073 (HN), src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

## What you actually need to do

- **Build the Cash rows in your planning sheet.**
  Opening balance → monthly burn → runway in months. Tie opening balance and burn to cumulative cash on the main tab; headcount and triggers live in [2.2](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md).
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

- **Calculate true monthly burn before any hire.**
  All cash out each month: payroll fully loaded, contractors, software, marketing, taxes, insurance, and operational spend. Salary alone is not burn — payroll compounds every month and locked-in recurring cost is what shortens runway.
  <!-- sources: src-067 (practitioner blog) -->

- **Before every hire, run a runway before/after table.**
  Cash on hand, monthly burn, runway in months — update the Cash rows within a working day. If you cannot answer "what happens to cash if they start next month?", the offer waits.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution), src-067 (practitioner blog) -->

- **Update the Cash tab monthly** at minimum; **review cash weekly when runway is under 12 months** or cash is lumpy (payroll, tax, large invoices).
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution), src-005 (HN) -->

- **Map payroll timing with your accountant.**
  When salary, employer costs, and tax actually leave the bank — so the forecast shows when cash moves, not accrual fiction. NL payroll detail: see [3.1](../people/3.1--first-hires.md) / [4.5](../legal/4.5--first-hires.md).
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

- **When runway drops below six months with no path back in 90 days**, execute the pre-agreed response from your [2.2 trigger → action list](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md) — do not wait for a crisis meeting.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

- **Share cash in bank, burn, and runway with the team** at ~7–10 people.
  Early employees took risk; hiding the numbers erodes trust and hides problems others could spot.
  <!-- sources: src-073 (HN) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

- **You don't know burn rate to within ~20%.**
  You are approving hires and spend without a reliable cash picture — bank balance alone is misleading once payroll runs.
  <!-- sources: src-005 (HN) -->

- **Bank or venture debt line counted in runway.**
  The facility hides how fast you burn through both pools — plan as if undrawn debt does not exist.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution), src-068 (First Round / Rob Hayes, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

- **A hire signed without modeling runway before/after.**
  You only noticed months of runway disappeared after the offer.
  <!-- sources: src-067 (practitioner blog) -->

- **You can't answer "what happens to cash if we hire X next month?" within a working day.**
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution) -->

### Founder / key-person time

- **A bad hire at ~12 months runway cost 2–3 months of founder distraction** before you cut — enough to jeopardize the company when runway is short.
  <!-- sources: src-072 (HN) -->

- **Only the CEO holds the cash numbers.**
  Everyone else optimizes locally while collective burn compounds.
  <!-- sources: src-065 (practitioner contribution), src-073 (HN) -->

### Process entry

- **You think you know burn but cannot state exact monthly cash out** — hiring decisions are being made in the dark.
  <!-- sources: src-067 (practitioner blog) -->

## Context variants

### If you are bootstrapped

Weekly cash review matters more — every hire must pass a runway floor after fully loaded cost. Do not count optimistic revenue in survival math; the downside rows in [2.2](../financial/2.2--first-hires.md) are not optional.

### If you have angel or friends & family investors

Share cash, burn, and runway with the team as you would internally with co-founders. A short runway section in your update mirrors the Cash rows — do not maintain a separate number set.

### If you are venture-backed (seed / Series A)

Exclude venture debt from core runway; build an 18-month cash plan right after a round closes and share it (no debt line, no revenue assumptions) with lead investors for accountability. Align sub-six-month responses with your board before you need them.

### If you are venture-backed (growth / PE)

Not typical at first hires — if you are already here, the same Cash rows and weekly-when-tight discipline apply until a finance hire owns forecasting.
