# Privacy & IP — Early Revenue

> By early revenue, privacy stops being theoretical: the question is whether your data setup would survive a professional investor's scrutiny, or trigger a "rebuild first" before they invest.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **Your data architecture and privacy setup would survive scrutiny from a professional investor.**
  A clear lawful basis, data that isn't scattered, DPAs with your processors, and a versioned privacy policy — the opposite of duct-tape. Trademarks and domains sit on the renewal calendar. The [first-hires baseline](4.6--first-hires.md) (know your data, reviewed policies, DPAs) is now diligence-ready.
  <!-- sources: src-041 (startup legal interview, human:stefanverkerk) -->

## What you actually need to do

1. **Run a DPA audit against your live stack** — hosting, email, analytics, support, payments — and close gaps before due diligence asks.
   <!-- sources: src-041 (startup legal interview, human:stefanverkerk) -->

2. **Version your privacy and cookie policies** — date each publish, keep prior versions on file, and update when you add customer data uses or new processors.
   <!-- sources: src-041 (startup legal interview, human:stefanverkerk) -->

3. **Put trademarks and domains on a renewal calendar** with owner and date — lapses are silent and painful to fix mid-fundraise.
   <!-- sources: src-038 (Practitioner contribution — Legal & Corporate Filing Structure) -->

4. **Draw a one-page data map:** what customer personal data you hold, where it lives, lawful basis, retention — not scattered across three people's heads.
   <!-- sources: src-041 (startup legal interview, human:stefanverkerk) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

- **Your data architecture and privacy setup would not survive scrutiny from a professional investor — data scattered, no clear lawful basis, duct-tape everywhere.**
  Outcome is often "rebuild or we are not investing."
  <!-- sources: src-041 (startup legal interview, human:stefanverkerk) -->

### Process entry

- **You added a customer-facing product feature but nobody updated the privacy policy or checked processor DPAs.**
  Policy and stack drift together — investors notice the gap.
  <!-- sources: src-041 (startup legal interview, human:stefanverkerk) -->

### Founder time

- **Due diligence sends a security questionnaire and founders spend two weeks reconstructing vendors and data flows.**
  The map should already exist.
  <!-- sources: src-041 (startup legal interview, human:stefanverkerk) -->

## How this evolves next

- **At growth, privacy moves from "diligence-ready" to active protection.**
  Internal access controls plus security-awareness training, a current processing register, registered trademarks, and explicit AI-use rules. See [4.6 — Growth](4.6--growth.md).
  <!-- sources: src-041 (startup legal interview, human:stefanverkerk) -->
