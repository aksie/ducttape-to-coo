# Internal Communication about Strategy — First Hires (2–10 people)

> At first hires the strategy story still travels by repetition in a small room — not by cascade machinery. That includes the **quarterly goals and current priorities** from [1.1](1.1--first-hires.md): if people only hear the narrative once and never hear this quarter's goals in the same rhythm, you get two mental models — "why we exist" and "what we're doing now" — that drift apart.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **Founders still tell the strategy story often enough to each employee, so that the early team shares one mental model.**
  Repeated narrative at this scale replaces formal cascade machinery — osmosis works when everyone is still in the same conversation often enough.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

- **Everyone can name this quarter's top priorities (and the primary growth metric) without looking them up.**
  Goals that live only in a kickoff doc are not communicated. Same bar as [1.1](1.1--first-hires.md): if you ask anyone what matters most this quarter, they should answer immediately.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

- **Weekly (or bi-weekly) team meetings include progress against those goals — not only task status.**
  What moved the priorities? What's blocking them? Where do we need help? Status without the goal filter is busywork theatre.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

- **When priorities change mid-quarter, founders say so explicitly and explain why.**
  Silent founder drift — acting on a new market view while the team still works the old list — is the communication failure mode at this size.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

## What you actually need to do

- **Keep current quarterly goals visible in one place the team already looks.**
  Pinned message, whiteboard, or one shared page — update it when the list changes. Setting the goals is [1.1](1.1--first-hires.md); keeping them in sight is this process.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

- **In the regular team meeting, open with the priorities — then status.**
  Two or three minutes: are we still on the quarter's goals? Then blockers and work. If the meeting never mentions the goals, people will treat them as optional.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

- **Repeat the strategy story in the same forums where you talk goals** — hiring conversations, weekly sync, and the quarter kickoff — so "why" and "what now" stay one narrative.
  Osmosis fails when the story is only told at all-hands-once-a-quarter and weekly meetings are pure tickets.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

- **At quarter start, close the last quarter out loud before setting the next:** how did we do, why, what changes — then the new goals.
  A written frank close (even short) beats a verbal rewrite; share it with the team, not only founders.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review), src-026 -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

- **Multi-founder teams explain direction slightly differently to their functions** — creating unspoken frustration and uncertainty before anyone names the misalignment.
  Inconsistent strategy stories compound as functions grow.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

- **Nobody can name the top priorities without checking a document** — or each person names a different list.
  The goals aren't driving decisions; communication stopped after the planning session.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

### Process entry

- **Internal comms is treated as separate from strategy and nobody owns transmission.**
  A practitioner view at this stage: internal comms is a part of the strategy process, not a separate parallel workstream — if nobody owns telling the story, cascade will fail when headcount grows.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) | flags: needs practitioner check -->

- **Goals exist in a document written at quarter start and are never mentioned in weekly meetings.**
  A goal that isn't spoken regularly is a wish. The meeting agenda is the transmission channel.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

## How this evolves next

At **early revenue** and **growth**, the story must flow down on a planning rhythm — not just up through reporting. See [1.2 at Growth](1.2--growth.md).
