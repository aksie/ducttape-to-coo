# Who Decides What — Early Revenue (~11–25 people)

> At early revenue you stop being the default approver on everything. Name a decision owner per area, write simple escalation thresholds, and make it explicit: founders give input — area owners decide.

## What still applies from earlier stages

The founder circle and approval list from [First Hires](1.4--first-hires.md) still govern among founders and for Bucket C items (hiring approval, big spend, pivot):

- **ABC decision map** — domain owner decides after consult (A), discuss + tie-break (B), unanimous (C).
- **Named decider + duty to consult** before calling reversible calls.
- **One visible list** of what still needs founder OK vs what the hire or area lead decides alone.

Early revenue extends that pattern to **area owners** (product, engineering, sales/GTM, ops) — not only founders.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **Every major area has a named decision owner and written escalation thresholds** — who decides spend up to €X, customer exceptions, and local tradeoffs without founder OK. Founders agree the thresholds in a founder sync; area leads operate inside them.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), processes.json 1.4 stageFocus -->

- **Founders give input in meetings; the area owner decides** — decisions should not re-open because a founder was not in the room. If the founder wasn't there, the owner still owns the call after consulting affected people.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), HSG seed article -->

- **Reversible decisions sit with the domain owner** — they listen, consult, then decide in their style. Local, course-correctable bets (e.g. a reversible €30k spend in one team) should not escalate to founders by default.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-437 -->

- **Roles carry genuine decision authority, not just task ownership** — the owner can decide against dissent and own the outcome: *"I hear you, but I'm deciding — it's my responsibility."* Task owners without authority create endless alignment loops.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-434 -->

- **You classify decisions by reversibility** — light process for two-way doors; take longer (and involve more people) for one-way doors. This becomes explicit Type 1 / Type 2 at growth — start naming the categories now.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

- **Structurally opposed incentives stay on different people when possible** — e.g. pricing (P) vs volume marketing (Q) should not collapse into one head when both mandates degrade. They must talk well; they need not merge.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-442 -->

## What you actually need to do

1. **Write one page: named owner per area + escalation thresholds** — product, engineering, sales/GTM, ops/finance. For each: what they decide alone, what hits the founder approval list, what needs cross-founder or exec alignment. Show the team the escalation subset; keep nuance among leaders.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), reStruggle ~15–25 -->

2. **Run meetings with founder input, area-owner decision** — in the room: gather dissent, then the area owner states the decision and who needs to know. Codify within 48h per [1.3 Communication Norms](1.3--first-hires.md).
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

3. **Introduce reversibility labels before you need Type 1/2 at scale** — mark examples of two-way vs one-way doors in your escalation doc (local vendor vs multi-year contract; pricing test vs company-wide price lock).
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

4. **Assign missions, not permanent identity, when standing up a new lead** — interim scope ("six-month mission to stand up sales ops") eases later role changes; title-as-identity blocks splits you will need at growth.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-443 -->

5. **Formalise when you add the page, not when you're already in conflict** — trigger: decisions get re-opened weekly, or two teams block each other on the same priority. That is the signal for named owners + thresholds, not a headcount rule alone.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), reStruggle ~15–25 -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

- **Decisions get re-litigated because a founder wasn't in the meeting** — the team learns to wait for the founder who was absent instead of accepting the area owner's call.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), processes.json 1.4 stageFocus -->

- **"But it used to work — why doesn't it now?"** — teams split but nobody owns coordinated moves across them; platform or cross-team outcomes fall between cracks. Nostalgia for the one-room phase is a signal to name deciders, not to re-merge teams.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-445 -->

### Founder / key-person time

- **Founder still has effective veto on every hire after naming a hiring manager** — candidates and recruiters lose context when every offer funnels back for a late culture veto. See [3.2 Recruitment & Hiring](../people/3.2--early-revenue.md) for the approval chain.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), atom-448 -->

### Process entry

- **Premature RACI, Holacracy, or heavy project methodology** — fixing structure before the room stops working. Extract principles; don't import full rulebooks yet.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo), src-063 (synthesis) -->

## How this evolves next

- **At growth**, C-level leaders play for the company not their function; Type 1/2 decision processes and integrative objection become explicit — see [1.4 at Growth](1.4--growth.md).
