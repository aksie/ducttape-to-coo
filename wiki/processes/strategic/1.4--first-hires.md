# Who Decides What — First Hires

> At first hires, decision rights are two conversations and one list: founders agree who owns what, then the team gets one visible list of what still needs founder OK. Everything else defaults to the person doing the work — who consults before they close.

**These ideas hold at every stage. First hires only changes the mechanics:**

1. **Accountability and authority travel together.** Whoever owns an outcome can decide on it — otherwise the team waits, and you end up back inside decisions you delegated.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->
2. **Decide who decides before you need it.** Rules written mid-conflict read as a power move; the same rules written in calm are just a map you look up.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis) -->
3. **Consult, then decide — don't decide by committee.** One named person owns the call and has to gather input from the people affected and the people with relevant expertise first. Gathering input is not the same as needing everyone to agree.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis) -->
4. **Formalise only when the informal version visibly breaks.** Process added ahead of the pain costs more than it saves — and at 2–10 people there is nothing structural to fix yet.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## What good looks like at this stage

- **Founders have aligned on default domain ownership before imposing rules on the team.** An Areas-of-Responsibility pass (skill + passion per domain, not job title) produces "Founder A defaults on X / Founder B defaults on Y."
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), Track B (Ada Lin AOR, Buechler founder sync) -->

- **One visible founder approval list exists.** Hiring (see [3.2 Recruitment & Hiring](../people/3.2--first-hires.md)), spend above an agreed €X, and material pivots or strategy changes need founder OK. Everything else, the owner of the work decides — and escalates only when impact is unclear.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), processes.json 1.4 stageFocus -->

- **Meetings name a decider and a deadline, and disagreement closes out loud.** One directly responsible person, not a matrix: "Alex decides by Friday." On the day-to-day calls the domain owner owns (Bucket A, once you write the map in action 3), the owner states the decision clearly and the non-owner commits aloud with a reopen trigger — *"I've said my piece. Your call. I back it until [date]."*
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), Advice process, Coinbase DRI, Grove, blog draft who-decides-when-founders-disagree -->

- **The team decides in one room — cross-functional trade-offs happen in real time, not through a matrix.** Founders or a small handful sit at one table; on a multi-sided business, content, monetisation, and marketing micro-trade-offs happen constantly because the same people hold all sides. Coordination stays invisible: there is nothing to coordinate between teams, because there are no teams yet.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

- **The culture works because hires tolerate ambiguity.** Self-starters who consult, decide, and move without a written RACI row — the grid naming who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed for each task — keep one-room speed; process-dependent hires stall it. Speed comes from talking, not documenting — at this size that is a feature, not a gap.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## What you actually need to do

1. **Run an Areas-of-Responsibility exercise among founders.** List domains (product, engineering, sales/GTM, ops/finance, hiring). Each founder scores skill (1–10) and passion; cluster ownership — if skill ties, passion wins. Output: default owners, not a CEO negotiation.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), First Round Founder Dating Playbook (Ada Lin) -->

2. **Capture a personal runway snapshot per founder (founders only).** Months without salary, minimum income to stay full-time, full-time trigger — not company runway ([2.1 Cash Flow](../financial/2.1--foundation.md)). Explicitly agree on how personal runway sets a company constraint on hiring pace, founder salary, and bootstrap vs raise. Keep it on the founder-sync agenda rather than in the hallway — see [3.6 — Foundation](../people/3.6--foundation.md) for that rhythm.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), YC Q7, Wasserman, Mercury cofounder prenup -->

3. **Write one page: approval list + ABC decision map.** Bucket A — domain owner decides after consult. Bucket B — discuss, then CEO tie-break at two founders. Bucket C — unanimous (equity, fundraising, hire approval, spend above €X, pivot). Show the team the Bucket C subset; keep the full map among founders. Template in the [who-decides proposal](../../../docs/proposals/who-decides-what-summary-and-direction.md#optional-team-template-first-hires). Align Bucket C with what your articles and shareholders' agreement (SHA) already reserve to shareholders — director pay, loans, related-party deals, share issues — see [Director vs shareholder decisions](../legal/director-and-shareholder-decisions.md).
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), Ascent ABC, Ramp founders agreement -->

4. **Delegate projects, not tasks.** Leadership sets boundaries; the team owns execution inside them — do not become the approver on every step. Practise letting go on reversible calls; keep founder approval on irreversible or high-stakes items only.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), Shape Up Ch. 10, Jason Fried -->

5. **Before a fork, answer Grove's six questions out loud.** What decision? By when? Who decides? Who must be consulted? Who can veto? Who needs to know? Then: free discussion → clear decision → full support.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), Grove High Output Management -->

6. **Codify decisions that matter within ~48 hours** per [1.3 Communication Norms](../strategic/1.3--first-hires.md) — chat announces, wiki/doc/ticket is authoritative. Decision rights and communication norms fail together when everything stays oral.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), 1.3 first-hires -->

7. **Hire people who operate when things are vague.** Screen for self-starters who can act inside loose boundaries — not people who stall until process is written down.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Founder / key-person time

- **Founders become bottlenecks on trivial decisions** — every vendor, design tweak, or hire still waits for founder OK. The team learns to wait instead of act.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), HN Founder Mode Tradeoff (Kent Beck) -->

- **The same fork gets argued for weeks** while a hire, a customer, or the runway clock waits. Trust erodes; later you blame "personality" when it was process ambiguity.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), blog draft who-decides-when-founders-disagree -->

### Process entry

- **The team waits for founder OK instead of acting** on work inside their mandate — or hiring and spend happen **outside** the approval list you never wrote down. When someone is responsible but cannot decide, it looks like "the system doesn't work" — the real issue is decision rights, not the org chart.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), HN CEO micromanager thread, src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

- **Roles were assumed from titles and never discussed** — or personal runway was never spoken aloud, so one founder stops or freelances after a few months and the other reads it as lack of commitment.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), Track B warning signs -->

- **Premature RACI, or project methodology appears before the one-room model breaks.** Founders confuse process activity with alignment; rule-keeping replaces talking. Principles from those frameworks can help later.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

### Output quality

- **The decider calls it without consulting** — the partner feels overruled, not committed. "Disagree and commit" used as cover for skipping input.
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), Advice process, Wistia counter-school -->

## How this evolves next

- **At early revenue, name a decision owner per area and add escalation thresholds.** Founders give input; the area owner decides. Formal memos and matrices wait for growth or repeated cross-team conflict — see [1.4 — Early Revenue](1.4--early-revenue.md).
  <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), HSG seed article, reStruggle -->

- **The inflection is when it no longer fits in one room.** The coordinating function that was invisible vanishes as teams split — you only notice once cross-functional trade-offs need someone above the functions. That is when to evolve toward [1.4 — Growth](../strategic/1.4--growth.md), where someone owns outcomes that cross teams.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->
