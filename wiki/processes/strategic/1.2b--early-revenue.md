# Shareholder & Investor Reporting — Early Revenue

> The monthly email habit from [first hires](1.2b--first-hires.md) continues. Early revenue adds board rhythm, cap table hygiene, and — once outside directors exist — a clear shared picture of what the board is *for*: conversation and decisions, not a status readout.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **Monthly investor updates still go out on calendar cadence** — same template, honest lookback — now to angels, seed investors, or an informal board.
  <!-- sources: src-058 (practitioner contribution, human:stefanverkerk), src-059 (template synthesis) -->

- **Board or advisor meetings have materials sent days ahead** — not slides assembled in the room — and the meeting itself is mostly conversation and decision-making, not a read-through of numbers the board could have absorbed from the pre-read.
  <!-- sources: src-058 (human:stefanverkerk), src-094 (TechCrunch), src-096 (Kjartan Rist / Forbes, vc_portfolio_interest), processes.json 1.2b stageFocus -->

- **Cap table is current and reconcilable** — options, SAFEs, and side letters don't live only in a lawyer's inbox.
  <!-- sources: src-058 (human:stefanverkerk) -->

- **You and your board share an explicit understanding that outside directors hold real decision authority once seated** — not an advisory role you can override informally. Control follows the cap table and bylaws, not goodwill.
  <!-- sources: src-088 (Jerry Neumann, vc_industry_veteran) -->

- **A healthy investor relationship applies pressure when you need it and eases off when you don't** — it isn't a fixed adversarial stance in either direction.
  <!-- sources: src-091 (David Paul, vc_investor_self_interest) -->

- **Board composition matches the capability areas the business must excel in, and stays close to five members (odd, for decisive votes)** — rather than growing because every investor in a round expects a seat.
  <!-- sources: src-096 (Kjartan Rist / Forbes, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

- **You treat the closing window of a round as when hard board alignment actually happens** — role changes, reporting fixes, governance terms. Formal board power doesn't disappear after the round, but *practical* leverage to compel change usually peaks at the negotiating table; once the money is committed, a split board can disagree without forcing a fix until performance makes the reckoning unavoidable.
  <!-- sources: src-112 (impact-VC partner interview, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

## What you actually need to do

1. **Keep the monthly [investor update](../../../templates/rendered/investor-update.html)** — add quarterly runway/burn/scenario sections once per quarter as in the template.
   <!-- sources: src-058 (human:stefanverkerk), src-059 -->

2. **Maintain cap table in one system of record** (Carta, Ledgy, spreadsheet with legal sign-off) — update within a week of any issuance or conversion.
   <!-- sources: src-058 (human:stefanverkerk) -->

3. **Loop legal in before any shareholder-facing action** — new round, option pool, director change, or side letter — not after signatures.
   <!-- sources: src-058 (human:stefanverkerk), processes.json 1.2b stageFocus -->

4. **Stage board-meeting prep on a timeline and send materials at least three days ahead — preferably seven:** rough outline three weeks out, draft content two weeks out, finalized pack one week out, pre-read circulated the Friday before. Include asks for the meeting, not only metrics.
   <!-- sources: src-097 (The Juice HQ), src-093 (Zack Urlocker), src-058 (human:stefanverkerk) -->

5. **Target roughly 90% accuracy on the financial statements in your board pack** rather than holding the pack back for false-precision 100% accuracy.
   <!-- sources: src-093 (Zack Urlocker) -->

6. **Never let the board meeting be the first time a board member hears material news** — anything significant should already be known before they walk in. Presenting to your board the same way you present internally doesn't work; board communication needs a deliberate reset.
   <!-- sources: src-097 (The Juice HQ) -->

7. **Optionally: run a quarterly "not-a-board-meeting" checkpoint with investors and advisors, even before you have a formal board.** It's the cheapest way to practice synthesizing the business into a focused narrative and running a discussion, not a status readout — and that skill compounds more if you start before Series A or B than if you wait for formality.
   <!-- sources: src-092 (Rishi Taparia / Garuda Ventures, vc_investor_self_interest), src-095 (NFX, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

8. **Push for a trusted, neutral industry expert on the board, rather than have your investor sitting on it indefinitely** — so you can treat that person, not the investor, as your natural first call. Founders go to a credible, conflict-free operator faster than to the investor who has an obvious economic stake in the answer.
   <!-- sources: src-112 (impact-VC partner interview, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

9. **Treat a proactive check-in call from your investor between scheduled meetings as normal, and answer straight rather than in pitch-mode.** That habit does more for early problem-surfacing than the monthly update alone.
   <!-- sources: src-112 (impact-VC partner interview, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

- **Board meetings are the first time investors see the numbers** — no pre-read, no consistent metrics month to month.
  <!-- sources: src-058 (human:stefanverkerk) -->

- **Your board pack has a full treatise on how an OKR should be defined, but nowhere states an actual key result or who owns it.** If an experienced outside board member can't find the target and the owner, the rest of the organization won't either — complexity is substituting for clarity, not demonstrating rigor. Same pattern as an 80–100 slide deck, just applied to goal-setting instead of reporting.
  <!-- sources: src-112 (impact-VC partner interview, vc_portfolio_interest) -->

- **Board meetings turn into public pressure or shaming when you miss numbers.** That degrades the quality of information the board will get from you going forward. The instinct to tighten control often isn't really about you — it's pressure cascading from investors' accountability to their LPs.
  <!-- sources: src-091 (David Paul, vc_investor_self_interest) -->

### Founder / key-person time

- **Shareholder questions get answered in ad hoc WhatsApp threads** instead of the monthly written record.
  <!-- sources: src-058 (human:stefanverkerk) -->

- **You use board-meeting time to justify your own performance instead of asking for help** — or you avoid sharing bad news out of fear of looking incompetent. Same Doer-in-Chief defensiveness as holding primary processes; by the time the board hears a real problem, it's too late for anyone to help. See also [1.5 Organizational Design](1.5--early-revenue.md).
  <!-- sources: src-095 (NFX, vc_portfolio_interest), src-091 (David Paul, vc_investor_self_interest) -->

### Process entry

- **Nobody can produce a current cap table without asking the lawyer.**
  <!-- sources: src-058 (human:stefanverkerk) -->

- **You assume "the board works for me" is literally true, or that the decision to stop being CEO will be your own call, once outside investors hold seats.** Once you have an institutional board, that decision is the board's far more often than founders are led to expect — don't assume your deal was different, don't see this as negative, but don't be naive.
  <!-- sources: src-087 (HN), src-088 (Jerry Neumann, vc_industry_veteran), src-089 (HN), src-090 (HN) -->

- **You and your board treat meetings mainly as a forum for reporting results** — or you're intimidated/star-struck by reputations in the room and forget it's still your meeting to run. A board that doesn't share an accurate picture of what it's for wastes time at best and creates problems at worst.
  <!-- sources: src-096 (Kjartan Rist / Forbes, vc_portfolio_interest), src-097 (The Juice HQ) -->

## How this evolves next

- **If you already ran informal quarterly checkpoints before your board existed, you'll be dramatically better prepared once a real board is seated at your next round.** The habit — not the formality — is what compounds.
  <!-- sources: src-092 (Rishi Taparia / Garuda Ventures, vc_investor_self_interest) -->

- At **growth**: structured board cadence, formal board packs, and emerging investor-relations discipline — see [1.2b at Growth](1.2b--growth.md).
