# Internal Communication about Strategy — Foundation (1–5 people)

> At this size, strategy communication *is* planning communication. Setting priorities lives in [1.1 Company-Level Planning](1.1--foundation.md); this page is how those priorities stay shared — visible, repeated, and updated out loud when they change.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **Everyone can name the top priorities for the quarter without checking a document.**
  Same bar as [1.1](1.1--foundation.md): if people cannot repeat the priorities, they were not communicated — a doc nobody opens does not count.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

- **Everybody understands why these are the priorities.**
  Not just the list — the reasoning. People who only hear "what" cannot judge tradeoffs when the founder is not in the room.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

- **When priorities change, you say so to the whole team and explain why.**
  Silent drift is the failure mode: one founder starts acting on a new view of the market while others still work the old list.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

- **The founder is not fielding daily "what should I work on?" questions.**
  If planning context lives only in the founder's head, every work-selection decision requires a conversation — that is a communication gap, not a motivation gap.
  <!-- sources: src-021 -->

## What you actually need to do

- **Make the 3–5 quarterly priorities impossible to miss.**
  Whiteboard, pinned Slack message, or single shared slide — pick one place people already look and keep it current. If they have to hunt, they will ignore them. The priorities themselves are set in [1.1](1.1--foundation.md).
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

- **Run a 15-minute weekly priorities check with the full team.**
  What did we get done? What's blocking us? Does our work still serve the quarterly priorities? Adjust if needed. No slides, no prep — this is the standup that keeps strategy alive, not a status theatre.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

- **With the founding team, discuss weekly or biweekly whether these are still the right priorities — and only act on other things once you've agreed a change.**
  Then tell the rest of the team the same day. Agreement among founders that never reaches the room is not communication.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

- **After each quarter, write a frank note: what went differently than planned, and next quarter's priorities — and send it to someone.**
  Even if that someone is only yourself. Verbal-only commitments are easy to rewrite in hindsight; a short written close makes the story durable.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

- **Ask anyone "what are we focused on right now?" and they hesitate.**
  Planning isn't working as communication yet — the priorities exist somewhere, but not in shared memory.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

- **You tell investors or your network different priorities than the ones written down for the team.**
  That usually means you think priorities should have changed, but you never made the change explicit inside the company.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

### Founder time

- **The founders constantly have to explain what people should work on.**
  The weekly check and the visible list are supposed to answer that; if they don't, the communication loop is broken.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

### Process entry

- **People work on things that aren't in the priorities because they didn't know the list existed or what it meant.**
  Not rogue behaviour — a transmission failure.
  <!-- sources: src-026 -->

## How this evolves next

- **At First Hires, keep repeating the strategy story** so the early team shares one mental model — osmosis still works, but you must own transmission on purpose. See [1.2 at First Hires](1.2--first-hires.md).
  <!-- sources: src-064 -->

- **The weekly full-team check stays**; as you grow toward ~10 people you will add clearer ownership of who tells the story when founders explain it differently to their functions.
  <!-- sources: src-064 -->
