# Who Decides What — Scaled (150+ people)

> At scaled stage, founder intuition still drives the company — suppressing it loses the role. Channel it with process steps instead of smoothing it away.

**Five ideas hold at every stage. Scaled only changes the mechanics:**

1. **Accountability and authority travel together.** Whoever owns an outcome can decide on it — otherwise the team waits, and you end up back inside decisions you delegated.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->
2. **Decide who decides before you need it.** Rules written mid-conflict read as a power move; the same rules written in calm are just a map you look up.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis) -->
3. **A decision you can walk back deserves less process than one you can't.** Cheap to reverse: one owner, light consult, move. Expensive or impossible to reverse: more people, more time, an explicit close.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-070 (serial-ceo) -->
4. **Consult, then decide — don't decide by committee.** One named person owns the call and has to gather input from the people affected and the people with relevant expertise first. Gathering input is not the same as needing everyone to agree.
   <!-- sources: src-063 (synthesis), src-070 (serial-ceo) -->
5. **Formalise only when the informal version visibly breaks.** Process added ahead of the pain costs more than it saves.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

**Carried over unchanged from earlier stages:** the ABC decision map, one visible list of what still needs founder or exec-team OK, and writing decisions that matter down within about 48 hours — the detail sits on [1.4 — First Hires](1.4--first-hires.md). Growth's executive mechanics all still hold at 150+: two decision paths depending on how expensive a call is to walk back, written per-role rules for what counts as a valid block, and executives who argue hard for their function and still decide for the company — see [1.4 — Growth](1.4--growth.md). Scaled replaces none of that; it adds a way to keep founder judgement in the system without letting it route around it.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **Process steps wrap founder intuition rather than replacing it** — a shower insight becomes a staged test, not a company-wide pivot by Monday.
  Founder energy drives the company; the operator's job is to channel it, not remove it.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

- **When conflict rises, use mediator-style neutral questions to surface what is unsaid.**
  Practitioner-trained mediation helps; it does not replace willingness to reflect. Your team may use other structured conflict tools — the point is surfacing what's unsaid before it explodes.

## Warning signs you're behind

### Founder / key-person time

- **An operator tries to smooth all founder erratic energy away** — and loses founder trust or the role.
  The energy that built the company needs somewhere to go.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

### Output quality

- **Task-driven, low-empathy founders who believe the best facts win and will not move without full conviction stall a growing org** — fine for product decisions, brutal for organisational disagreement.
  Mediation helps but does not replace willingness to reflect. Patterns vary — treat this as one failure mode to watch for, not a personality diagnosis.
