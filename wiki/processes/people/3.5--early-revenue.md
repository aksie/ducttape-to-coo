# Performance Management — Early Revenue

> Informal feedback stops scaling around fifteen people. Introduce a lightweight quarterly review rhythm, connect role goals to company OKRs, and make sure nothing in a formal check-in is news — that work happens in [3.6 1-on-1s](3.6--early-revenue.md) all quarter.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **You run quarterly team goals alongside regular manager 1-on-1s.**
  Goals set expectations and alignment; they are not a substitute for talking, and they are not a final performance rating by themselves.
  <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news), src-010 (firstround_review) -->

- **Feedback flows in real time between formal review cycles.**
  Quarterly check-ins summarize what people already know, not surprises. That requires goals, continuous feedback, and lightweight reviews working together — and weekly [3.6 1-on-1s](3.6--early-revenue.md) that log what was actually said.
  <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news), src-079 (human_experience), src-080 (openorg.fyi) -->

- **Scheduled performance conversations and written notes replace hallway-only feedback.**
  Above ~15 people, informal osmosis breaks down; managers capture outcomes and follow up.
  <!-- sources: src-076 (hacker_news) -->

- **Career coaching is separate from weekly 1-on-1s.**
  Status and blockers in the short meeting; growth, skills, and performance development in a dedicated quarterly, monthly, or bi-annual slot.
  <!-- sources: src-078 (firstround_review) | flags: vc_portfolio_interest -->

- **Individual goals connect to [company OKRs](../strategic/1.1--early-revenue.md) but OKR scores are decoupled from compensation and formal ratings.**
  Tying them together invites sandbagging and kills ambitious goals.
  <!-- sources: src-010 (firstround_review) -->

- **Each role has a short written picture of what "doing well" looks like.**
  Before review season, not invented in the review meeting. Subjective excellence is fine at this size; ambiguity about expectations is not.
  <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news) -->

- **Weekly 1-on-1s run from a shared standing agenda with a bidirectional Feedback slot.**
  Same template every week; reports add their items; one fixed section covers feedback from them to you and from you to them.
  <!-- sources: src-079 (human_experience) | flags: practitioner_first_person -->

## What you actually need to do

1. **Align quarterly personal, team, and company objectives** to your [1.1 planning](../strategic/1.1--early-revenue.md) rhythm — and keep [3.6 1-on-1s](3.6--early-revenue.md) for continuous feedback, not status-only standups disguised as management.
   <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news), src-076 (hacker_news) -->

2. **Publish a one-page role-expectations doc per function** — what good looks like this quarter, how it links to team goals, and examples of "meets" vs "exceeds" — share before anyone's review conversation. Keep it pragmatic at this stage.
   <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news) -->

3. **Run a quarterly or twice-yearly check-in (~30–60 minutes) built on brief self- and peer input** — e.g. continue / stop / start — then the manager states conclusions and next-quarter priorities. Optional light write-up; not a ten-page form.
   <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news), src-075 (hacker_news) -->

4. **Each report maintains a simple shared growth-goals doc**, updated before periodic career check-ins — wins, development areas, and next focus. A spreadsheet or doc beats an HRIS module at this headcount.
   <!-- sources: src-078 (firstround_review) | flags: vc_portfolio_interest -->

5. **Schedule the first formal performance conversation ~nine months after join, or if on a 7-month first contract: five months in — then at least yearly.**
  Don't wait a full year before the first documented check-in or salary discussion feels arbitrary.
   <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news) -->

6. **Log feedback in the 1-on-1 doc after every weekly meeting** — one shared note per report; write what you told them and what they told you. Reference it when you prep quarterly check-ins; follow up on growth commitments within three months.
   <!-- sources: src-079 (human_experience), src-080 (openorg.fyi), src-081 (hoffdigital.com) | flags: practitioner_first_person -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Output quality

- **Negative performance feedback appears for the first time in an annual review.**
  The manager avoided hard conversations for months; the employee experiences it as an ambush, not development.
  <!-- sources: src-077 (hacker_news) -->

- **Feedback turns personal ("hard to work with") instead of behavioral.**
  Without a review structure people get defensive and nothing changes; use specific examples and frameworks like SBI.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com) -->

### Founder / key-person time

- **Career progression and corrective feedback only happen in a once- or twice-yearly review.**
  You cannot wait that long; the strongest tool is still the weekly or bi-weekly 1-on-1.
  <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news) -->

- **Top performers' pay drifts below market because nobody formalized recognition.**
  They'll leave and you'll be shocked; reviews should force explicit comp conversations.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com) -->

### Process entry

- **You rolled out a heavy review template before you have a rhythm of talking, noting outcomes, and following up.**
  The form exists; nobody uses it consistently.
  <!-- sources: src-076 (hacker_news) -->

- **Formal review feedback was never written down in weekly 1-on-1 notes.**
  You reconstruct from memory; the report disputes whether you ever raised it. A log beats an argument about what was said months ago.
  <!-- sources: src-079 (human_experience) | flags: practitioner_first_person -->

- **You copied enterprise 360s or page-long rubrics at ~20 people.**
  Checkbox culture without behavior change; keep the cycle self-review, manager assessment, and one conversation.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com) -->

## How this evolves next

At **growth**, add a formal bi-annual or annual review cycle, manager training on feedback, compensation reviews tied to performance, and documented promotion criteria — see [3.5 at Growth](3.5--growth.md). Hire dedicated HR help around 35–50 people before the informal system breaks under legal and calibration load.
<!-- sources: src-076 (hacker_news) -->

## Tools & resources

- **Optional light peer input as needed through the year** — highlights contributions a manager-only view misses; not a mandatory enterprise 360 cycle.
  <!-- sources: src-075 (hacker_news) -->

- **Run the full process manually in Notion or Google Docs for one quarter** before you buy performance HRIS — prove the rhythm first, then automate what already works.
  <!-- sources: src-080 (openorg.fyi) -->

- **Self-review prompts before each check-in** — proudest win, hardest surprise, where you want to grow, what support would help (~30 minutes). Reflection, not a resume.
  <!-- sources: src-081 (hoffdigital.com) -->

- **Situation–Behaviour–Impact (SBI) script for hard feedback** — specific moment, observable action, real consequence; strips emotional labels from corrective conversations.
  <!-- sources: src-080 (openorg.fyi) -->
