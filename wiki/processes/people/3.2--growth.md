# Recruitment & Hiring — Growth (~50–150 people)

> At growth stage you start hiring senior leaders and C-level roles. The budget → offer → contract chain from [early revenue](3.2--early-revenue.md) still applies. Split interviews into three lenses (fit, potential, culture), **score don't veto**, and let the hiring manager decide.

## What good looks like at this stage

- **Senior hires go through three separate conversations — and no single interviewer outside the hiring manager has a veto.**
  The hiring manager checks fit and chemistry. Someone one level up checks potential only (live case — not a final hire/no-hire). A long-tenure culture carrier checks values over coffee. Each gives a signal (e.g. a 1–5 rating); none can kill the hire alone, and interviewers are told beforehand.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) | flags: needs practitioner check -->

- **The hire happens based on all three conversations — not when one person says "not a fit."**
  A binary culture veto from one interviewer is too subjective — that is not the question at this stage.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) | flags: needs practitioner check -->

- **Inputs are scores the hiring manager weighs — not a unanimity rule.**
  Example: stronger on culture, slightly less potential, fine for the role now — the hiring manager decides and owns the tradeoff.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

- **Culture non-negotiables are few and owned by the hiring manager** — not a blanket "no culture fit" anyone can invoke.
  The culture checker scores against explicit values dimensions (e.g. entrepreneurship 3/5, alignment 4/5) and is coached not to imply (non-)approval with extreme scores; the hiring manager interprets what matters for this role.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

- **A new leader gets the role immediately** — they get the room to make real decisions in their style from day one, with advice in the first weeks.
  Not months of partial projects while you retain the final call.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## What you actually need to do

1. **Run the three conversations in order — with different jobs for each round.** (1) **Hiring manager** — role fit, hard criteria, chemistry: did this meeting give you energy? (2) **One level up** — **potential only**: unprepared problem, live case. Output is a potential estimate, not hire/no-hire — **no veto**. (3) **Long-tenure culture carrier** — life, work, values over coffee. Also **no veto**. The three sparks together decide.
   <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) | flags: needs practitioner check -->

2. **Make explicit that a 3/5 culture score is a weighting input, not a kill switch** — trust a culture-literate hiring manager to own the tradeoff; tell interviewers upfront their score advises, it doesn't veto.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

3. **On an executive hire, hand over domain ownership from day one** — stutter-step onboarding (small shadow projects for months) is often the hirer's failure, not the hiree's. See [1.4 at Growth](../strategic/1.4--growth.md) for decision-rights handover.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

4. **Accept founder disillusion as the company scales** — people hired at 50+ are a different breed than founder types; specialists (legal, compliance, finance) won't feel like clones and shouldn't.
   <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## Warning signs you're behind

### Process entry

- **A culture or values interview ends in a binary "not a fit" with veto power.**
  One subjective no blocks senior hires that the hiring manager and executive team would otherwise make — and the process stops scaling with you.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

- **You expect a specialist hire (e.g. CRO, CFO, Head of CS) to be friends with every other department head.**
  The bar is manager chemistry and values alignment — not universal friendship across the leadership team.
  <!-- sources: src-064 (anonymous-practitioner) -->

- **Ambiguity over who the hiring manager is** — recruiters burn out chasing approvals; candidates drop when nobody clearly owns the decision.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

- **"We'll figure out the VP scope after they join"** — four months of project hopping, then "not fast enough."
  Scope and decision rights must be clear before offer — not discovered in month three.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

### Output quality

- **The hiring manager compromises on profile because the market is tight, then delivery gets blocked** — the system is blamed when the tradeoff was never owned explicitly.
  If you hire below the bar, the hiring manager must say so and accept the consequence.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

### Founder / key-person time

- **Founders re-enter late with an effective veto** — "we called so-and-so, we really shouldn't" funnels every hire back to a founder who no longer has context.
  Parallel: a former approver still asked to sign off on decisions they no longer understand.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

- **Founders structurally disillusioned as the company scales** — even the executives you need feel wrong because they aren't founder clones.
  If every senior hire feels like a misfit, the problem is often your expectations, not the candidates.
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## How this evolves next

- **The founder must hire the first ~50–100; then the decision right has to genuinely move to managers** — or hiring stalls.
  At scaled stage the same three-lens pattern extends into professional recruiting infrastructure (ATS, pipelines) — see [3.2 at Scaled](3.2--scaled.md).
  <!-- sources: src-070 (serial-ceo) -->

## Tools & resources

- **9-box thinking (performance × potential × values)** — optional shared language across the three conversations, not a bureaucracy layer.
  Gives hiring manager, senior reviewer, and culture carrier the same vocabulary without a single scorecard veto.
