# VC fund return benchmarks — sources & data

Founders considering venture capital hear a lot of round numbers for "what
returns VC funds need to make." This page collects what's actually publicly
published, by source, so claims can be checked rather than repeated as folk
wisdom. It backs the "What VC returns are really created?" section of
[Before You Raise Money — Understand the VC Model](../../blog/before-you-raise-money-understand-the-vc-model.html).

**Compiled from public reports as of mid-2026 — these are quarterly/annual
series, so figures will move. Re-check before citing a specific number.**

---

## Quick answer

- **US net IRR over a 10-year horizon** has landed around **13%–18%** in
  recent editions of the Invest Europe / Cambridge Associates benchmark that
  feeds the annual *State of European Tech* report.
- **European net IRR over the same 10-year horizon** has been higher —
  roughly **17%–21%** in the same editions — and has also outperformed
  European public equities over 10+ year horizons.
- **Dispersion between funds is wide.** Carta's US vintage data shows a
  90th-percentile fund clearing 20%+ net IRR for most 2017–2024 vintages,
  while the 75th percentile doesn't exceed ~15.5% for *any* vintage in that
  range — most of a fund's headline return sits with a small top slice.
- **Paper markups run ahead of realized cash.** TVPI (total value, paper +
  cash) has been rising across most US vintages; DPI (cash actually
  returned) remains thin, especially for 2019–2020 vintages still early in
  their return cycle. A fund "up" on paper hasn't necessarily returned
  anything yet.
- **EIF is not the direct source of the European IRR figures usually
  attributed to it.** The figures that circulate as "EIF benchmarks" mostly
  originate from Invest Europe / Cambridge Associates. See [§4](#4-eifs-own-published-benchmarks-a-different-thing) below —
  this is a common mix-up worth avoiding when citing sources.

## By source

### 1. Carta — VC Fund Performance, Q1 2026 (US-focused, quarterly)

Tracks 2,775 US VC funds closed 2017–Q1 2026 ($119.3B raised). The June 2026
release showed median net TVPI rising across nearly every vintage
2017–2024; 90th-percentile net IRR is above 20% for most vintages in that
range, but 75th-percentile IRR doesn't exceed 15.5% in any of them —
realized DPI remains thin, especially for the 2019–2020 vintages. Q1 2026
also saw $3.9B raised across 86 new funds, the strongest Q1 since 2022.

Source: [Carta — VC Fund Performance: Q1 2026](https://carta.com/data/vc-fund-performance-q1-2026/)

### 2. Cambridge Associates — US PE/VC Benchmark Commentary, 1H 2025 (published Jan 2026)

Pooled net-of-fee returns from a database of 2,699 US VC funds (1981–2025
vintages, $591B NAV). The US VC Index returned 6.4% in 1H 2025, continuing a
recovery after seven consecutive down quarters (Jan 2022–Sep 2023).
Vintage-year returns for 2015–2022 ranged from -2.5% to +8.6% in 1H 2025.
The full report also carries horizon IRRs (6mo/1yr/3yr/5yr/10yr/15yr/20yr/25yr)
benchmarked against public-market equivalents — the specific 10-year figure
wasn't pulled into this page; see the PDF.

Sources:
[Cambridge Associates — US PE/VC Benchmark Commentary: First Half 2025](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/insight/us-pe-vc-benchmark-commentary-first-half-2025/) ·
[full PDF](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2026-01-US-PE-VC-Benchmark-Commentary-1H2025-Data-PUBLIC.pdf)

### 3. Invest Europe / *State of European Tech* — US vs. Europe 10-year net IRR

The 10-year net IRR figures most often quoted for "European VC vs. US VC"
come from Invest Europe's Performance Benchmark Report, built on Cambridge
Associates methodology, and get reproduced in the annual *State of European
Tech* report. Recent editions cite European VC 10-year net IRR of
17.2%–20.77%, ahead of US VC's 13.1%–18.18% and ahead of European public
equities over 10+ year horizons.

Sources:
[Invest Europe — Performance of European Private Equity Benchmark Report 2023](https://www.investeurope.eu/news/newsroom/european-buy-outs-growth-venture-capital-infrastructure-performance-maintains-lead-over-public-market-returns/) ·
[Sifted — European VCs outperform US VCs over 10 and 15 year horizons](https://sifted.eu/articles/european-vc-irr-2024) ·
[State of European Tech 2025](https://www.stateofeuropeantech.com/)

### 4. EIF's own published benchmarks — a different thing

Worth flagging clearly: the fund-return figures that show up in *State of
European Tech* are sourced from Cambridge Associates and Invest Europe, **not
published directly by EIF**, even though EIF is closely associated with that
report. EIF's own return data comes from separate, non-recurring Research &
Market Analysis working papers (not a quarterly/annual series) — e.g. WP
2017/41 and WP 2011/09/2019/59 — using EIF's internal LP dataset on
EIF-backed funds (one sample covers ~3,600 investments, 1996–2015). These
are the actual EIF-authored numbers, and they haven't been pulled into this
page yet — the specific IRR figures need extracting from the working paper
PDF.

Source: [EIF Working Paper 2017/41 — The European venture capital landscape: an EIF perspective](https://www.fi-compass.eu/sites/default/files/publications/eif_wp_41.pdf)

## How to read these numbers

- **Pooled index vs. percentile-of-funds vs. point estimate are not the same
  question.** Cambridge Associates' 6.4% is one number for an entire pooled
  index over six months. Carta's 90th/75th percentile figures describe the
  *spread* across thousands of individual funds by vintage year. Don't
  average or compare these directly.
- **Vintage-year effects (the "J-curve").** A fund's IRR looks poor in its
  early years (fees paid, no exits yet) and improves as portfolio companies
  mature and exit. Recent vintages (2022–2024) will structurally look worse
  than 2015–2017 vintages for years yet, regardless of underlying quality —
  that's normal, not a warning sign on its own.
- **Net vs. gross.** All figures on this page are **net** (after fees and
  carry) unless stated otherwise — net is what an LP actually receives, and
  the fairer basis for founder-facing claims about "what a fund needs."
  Gross IRR figures (before fees/carry) run higher and shouldn't be compared
  to the net figures here.
- **Realized (DPI) vs. paper (TVPI).** TVPI includes unrealized, marked
  portfolio value; DPI is cash actually distributed. Carta's finding that
  DPI remains thin for 2019–2020 vintages is a caution against reading a
  rising TVPI as money already banked.
