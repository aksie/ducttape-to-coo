## B. Structure — principles first, five sections intact

### The shape

```
# Who Decides What — {Stage}
> blockquote: what changes at this stage (2–3 sentences, no jargon)

**Five ideas hold at every stage. {Stage} only changes the mechanics:**
1–5. one-line principles, each with its source comment

**Carried over unchanged from earlier stages:** one paragraph, links, one source comment

## What good looks like at this stage      ← ≤7 bullets, stage-specific only
## What you actually need to do            ← ≤7 bullets
## Warning signs you're behind             ← ≤7 bullets total, 3 categories
## How this evolves next
## Tools & resources
## Sources
```

### How this fits the section contract

- **The principles live in the intro, above the first `##`.** They are not a section, so the five-section order and the max-7-bullets-per-section rule are untouched. They are one-line numbered items — a bold clause plus one sentence — deliberately *not* `**bold claim** + 2–4 line explanation` bullets, so they read as a preamble rather than a competing claim list.
- **Source integrity is kept.** Each principle carries the source comment of the claim it generalises; all five are derived from existing approved claims, not invented. The carry-forward paragraph carries one combined comment for the claims folded into it.
- **`## What still applies from earlier stages` is removed.** Its two durable rules became principles 1 and 4; its three mechanics (ABC map, approval list, 48-hour codification) became the carry-forward paragraph with a link to first-hires. 1.4 @ growth now has the same shape as every other page in `wiki/processes/`.
- **Nothing in the app depends on that heading.** `js/wiki-content-score.js` scores only `## What you actually need to do` and `## Warning signs you're behind` (300-char threshold). Measured after the rewrite: **3670 chars, status 3 (complete)**.

### The five durable principles

1. **Accountability and authority travel together.** Whoever owns an outcome can decide on it. *(src-070; generalises growth carry-forward bullet 4, first-hires good-looks-like bullet 6, early-revenue good-looks-like bullet 4.)*
2. **Decide who decides before you need it.** Rules written mid-conflict read as a power move; written in calm they are just a map you look up. *(src-063.)*
3. **A decision you can walk back deserves less process than one you can't.** Cheap to reverse: one owner, light consult, move. Expensive or impossible to reverse: more people, more time, an explicit close. *(src-063, src-070. This is the sentence that now carries the weight the Type 1 / Type 2 labels used to carry — stated as a rule about process cost, not as a taxonomy.)*
4. **Consult, then decide — don't decide by committee.** One named person owns the call and must gather input from affected people and relevant expertise first. Gathering input is not the same as needing agreement. *(src-063, src-070.)*
5. **Formalise only when the informal version visibly breaks.** Process added ahead of the pain costs more than it saves. *(src-070; replaces four scattered "don't over-formalise" statements, so each stage keeps only its own symptom.)*

### How each stage narrows them

| Stage | Principle 1 becomes | 2 becomes | 3 becomes | 4 becomes | 5 becomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **first-hires** | Hires with a mandate; delegate projects, not tasks | ABC map + one visible approval list, written before the first fork | Founder approval stays on the calls you can't walk back | Named decider + deadline in meetings; consult → decide → commit script | Nothing structural to fix yet; hire people who work in ambiguity |
| **early-revenue** | Area owners can decide against dissent and own the result | Named owner per area + written escalation thresholds | Local, course-correctable spend stops escalating to founders | Founders give input; area owner decides — no re-opening because a founder was absent | Trigger is re-opened decisions or two teams blocking each other, not headcount |
| **growth** *(applied)* | Executive role handed over on day one, with real rights | Written per-role rules for what counts as a valid block | Two decision paths — walk-back-able calls sit with the role owner, expensive-to-reverse bets run a shared process | "Who did you check with?" coaching until it's the norm | Take the principle from a framework, not the rulebook |
| **scaled** | (stub) | (stub) | (stub) | Structured conflict tools; process wraps founder intuition | (stub) |
